# Symptom scale benchmark scores

Notes for judging whether a reported score on the depression and anxiety scales used by the European catalogue sits in a normal or a clinical range. All figures below were taken from the cited pages. Where a table was recovered from PDF text extraction, that is said explicitly. Higher scores are worse on every scale here.

## PHQ-9 (Patient Health Questionnaire-9)

### Takeaway
The PHQ-9 is a 9-item DSM-based depression severity scale scored 0–27. The bands clinicians use are 0–4 none/minimal, 5–9 mild, 10–14 moderate, 15–19 moderately severe, and 20–27 severe, with ≥10 as the usual screen for major depression. A 5-point drop is the widely quoted individual change threshold, but it is not the only published figure.

### Cited Findings
- Full name: Patient Health Questionnaire-9, the depression module of the Patient Health Questionnaire. Nine items, each 0 (“not at all”) to 3 (“nearly every day”) over the last two weeks. Total 0–27. Higher = worse. — [Kroenke, Spitzer, and Williams, 2001, Journal of General Internal Medicine](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1495268/)
- Citation: Kroenke K, Spitzer RL, Williams JBW. The PHQ-9: validity of a brief depression severity measure. Journal of General Internal Medicine. 2001;16(9):606–613. DOI: 10.1046/j.1525-1497.2001.016009606.x. US primary care and obstetrics-gynaecology clinics, data collected 1997–1999. — [same paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1495268/)
- Severity bands used in the analyses: 0–4, 5–9, 10–14, 15–19, and 20 or greater. Scores of 5, 10, 15, and 20 are the lower edges of mild, moderate, moderately severe, and severe depression. The cut points were chosen because they are easy to remember; other cuts did not change the construct-validity pattern. — [Kroenke et al., 2001](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1495268/)
- Screening: against a mental-health-professional interview in 580 primary care patients, PHQ-9 ≥10 had sensitivity 88% and specificity 88% for major depression. Prevalence of major depression in that interview sample was 7% (41/580). Operating characteristics at neighbouring cuts are in their Table 3 (≥9 through ≥15). — [Kroenke et al., 2001](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1495268/)
- What the bands meant in that interview sample: scores under 5 almost always meant no depressive disorder; 5–9 was mostly no depression or subthreshold depression; 10–14 was mixed; 15 or greater usually meant major depression. Mean (SD): major depression 17.1 (6.1), n=41; other depressive disorder 10.4 (5.4), n=65; no depressive disorder 3.3 (3.8), n=474. — [Kroenke et al., 2001](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1495268/)
- Test-retest in the same paper: clinic PHQ-9 and a telephone PHQ-9 within 48 hours correlated 0.84, with mean scores 5.08 and 5.03. Cronbach’s alpha 0.89 (primary care) and 0.86 (obstetrics-gynaecology). — [Kroenke et al., 2001](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1495268/)
- The Pfizer/Kroenke instruction sheet restates the same scheme: cuts of 5, 10, and 15 are mild, moderate, and severe on the PHQ-9 (and on the GAD-7); ≥10 is a “yellow flag” and ≥15 a “red flag.” — [PHQ and GAD-7 instruction manual](https://manual.jointcommission.org/pub/BHCInstruments/RomInstr00054/instructions_PHQ.pdf)
- European general-population means: nationally representative German household surveys, 2003–2008, n=5018, ages ≥14, mean age 48.9 (SD 18.1). Women PHQ-9 mean (SD) 3.1 (3.5); men 2.7 (3.5). “Moderate to high severity” prevalence 5.6%. — [Kocalevent, Hinz, and Brähler, 2013, General Hospital Psychiatry](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.genhosppsych.2013.04.006)
- Citation: Kocalevent R-D, Hinz A, Brähler E. Standardization of the depression screener Patient Health Questionnaire (PHQ-9) in the general population. General Hospital Psychiatry. 2013;35(5):551–555. DOI: 10.1016/j.genhosppsych.2013.04.006.
- German clinical sample that matches iFightDepression’s mild-to-moderate band (inclusion PHQ-9 5–14): baseline PHQ-9 mean (SD) 9.1 (3.6) in the iFightDepression arm (n=173) and 9.7 (3.3) in the control arm (n=173). — [Oehler et al., 2020, Journal of Medical Internet Research](https://www.jmir.org/2020/7/e15361/)
- Minimal important change: in 434 intervention patients from the IMPACT late-life depression trial (63% female, mean age 71), the PHQ-9 minimal clinically important difference for an individual, estimated as two standard errors of measurement from 7-day retests, was 5 points on the 0–27 scale. — [Löwe, Unützer, Callahan, Perkins, and Kroenke, 2004, Medical Care](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15550799/)
- Citation: Löwe B, Unützer J, Callahan CM, Perkins AJ, Kroenke K. Monitoring depression treatment outcomes with the Patient Health Questionnaire-9. Medical Care. 2004;42(12):1194–1201. DOI: 10.1097/00005650-200412000-00006.
- Conflicting later estimates, same instrument: a 2021 Journal of Clinical Epidemiology analysis of two depression trials (n=1122) defined the minimal clinically important difference as the change giving a 50% chance the patient “feels better” (ED50). Averaged across patients this was 3.7 points (23%) on the PHQ-9, but it ranged from no change at very mild baseline severity up to 14 points at very high severity. The same paper states that English primary-care psychological-therapy services were using a 6-point PHQ-9 change and a 4-point GAD-7 change, derived from a reliable-change index. — [Journal of Clinical Epidemiology, 2021, PMC8485844](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8485844/)

### Inferences
- A catalogue score under 5 is in the band Kroenke treated as no depressive disorder. A score of 10–14 is the “gray zone” in which major depression is possible but not the usual finding. A score of 15 or more is the band in which major depression was usual in the 2001 interview sample.
- German general-population means (about 3) sit in the 0–4 band. The iFightDepression trial’s own baseline PHQ-9 of about 9 sits at the top of the mild band, which matches that trial’s inclusion rule of 5–14.
- For an individual, a change of 5 points is the figure most often treated as meaningful. It comes from measurement error in older US primary-care patients, not from a European youth or student sample. A service that uses the English therapy-service rule will call reliable improvement at 6 points instead.

### Gaps
- No university-student PHQ-9 mean (the GetStarted population) was retrieved. The German norms start at age 14 but are not a student sample, and they are not broken out here for ages 15–25.
- Kocalevent’s phrase “moderate to high severity” (5.6%) was not tied, in the abstract, to an explicit numeric cut. In Kroenke’s labels that phrase corresponds to scores of 10 or more, but that mapping is an inference.
- The 2021 ED50 paper’s author list was not captured from the page text.

## PHQ-4, including PHQ-2 and GAD-2

### Takeaway
The PHQ-4 total runs from 0 to 12. The bands Kroenke published, and the ones that match a split into normal / mild / moderate / severe, are 0–2 normal, 3–5 mild, 6–8 moderate, and 9–12 severe. Tankevirus’s 13% / 33% / 27% / 27% split uses those four bands. PHQ-2 and GAD-2 are the two-item halves, each 0–6, and a score of 3 or more on either half is the usual positive screen.

### Cited Findings
- The PHQ-4 is the PHQ-2 (two depression items) plus the GAD-2 (two anxiety items). Stem: “Over the last 2 weeks, how often have you been bothered by the following problems?” Each item 0–3. Total 0–12. Higher = worse. — [Kroenke, Spitzer, Williams, and Löwe, 2009, Psychosomatics](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0033318209708643)
- Citation: Kroenke K, Spitzer RL, Williams JBW, Löwe B. An ultra-brief screening scale for anxiety and depression: the PHQ-4. Psychosomatics. 2009;50(6):613–621. DOI: 10.1016/S0033-3182(09)70864-3. PMID: 19996233.
- Bands stated in that paper: normal (0–2), mild (3–5), moderate (6–8), severe (9–12). In their US primary-care sample the mean PHQ-4 was 2.5 (SD 2.8): 61.5% normal, 23.3% mild, 10.6% moderate, 4.6% severe. Mean age 47.2 (SD 15.4); 66% women. Mean PHQ-8 in the same sample was 5.1 (SD 5.2) and mean GAD-7 was 5.3 (SD 5.3). — [Kroenke et al., 2009](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0033318209708643)
- Item order in the reproduced form: items 1–2 are anxiety (feeling nervous; not being able to stop worrying) and sum to the GAD-2 (0–6); items 3–4 are depression (little interest; feeling down) and sum to the PHQ-2 (0–6). On each subscale, 3 or greater is a positive screen. — [PHQ-4 scoring sheet citing Kroenke et al., 2009](https://www.utahmed.org/Common/Uploaded%20files/Docs/CS_Resources/PHQ-4%20Patient%20Health%20Questionnaire.pdf); same scoring on the [NINDS instrument page](https://cde-fe.ninds.nih.gov/ninds/noc-report/F2495/Patient%20Health%20Questionnaire%20-%204%20(PHQ-4))
- PHQ-2 validation: two items, total 0–6. In 580 patients with a mental-health-professional interview, PHQ-2 ≥3 had sensitivity 83% and specificity 92% for major depression. The authors identified 3 as the optimal screening cut. Same 6,000-patient primary-care and obstetrics-gynaecology pool as the PHQ-9 paper. — [Kroenke, Spitzer, and Williams, 2003, Medical Care](https://doi.org/10.1097/01.mlr.0000093487.78664.3c)
- Citation: Kroenke K, Spitzer RL, Williams JBW. The Patient Health Questionnaire-2: validity of a two-item depression screener. Medical Care. 2003;41(11):1284–1292.
- GAD-2: the instruction manual says a score of 3 or greater on the PHQ-2 or the GAD-2 should lead to the full PHQ-9 and/or GAD-7 plus a clinical interview. — [PHQ and GAD-7 instruction manual](https://manual.jointcommission.org/pub/BHCInstruments/RomInstr00054/instructions_PHQ.pdf)
- A 2010 review of the PHQ family states that the optimal cut is ≥10 on the PHQ-9 and GAD-7 and ≥3 on the PHQ-2 and GAD-2. — [Kroenke, Spitzer, Williams, and Löwe, 2010, General Hospital Psychiatry, PMID 20633738](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20633738/)
- Operating characteristics quoted for the GAD-2 at ≥3, from Kroenke et al., Annals of Internal Medicine, 2007;146:317–325: sensitivity 86% and specificity 83% for generalised anxiety disorder; also reported for panic (76%/81%), social anxiety (70%/81%), and PTSD (59%/81%). These percentages are taken from a secondary page that cites that paper, not from the 2007 PDF itself. — [National HIV Curriculum GAD-2 page](https://www.hiv.uw.edu/page/mental-health-screening/gad-2)
- Citation for that GAD-2 source, as named on the secondary page: Kroenke K, Spitzer RL, Williams JB, Monahan PO, Löwe B. Anxiety disorders in primary care: prevalence, impairment, comorbidity, and detection. Annals of Internal Medicine. 2007;146:317–325.

### Inferences
- Tankevirus’s four percentages (13% normal, 33% mild, 27% moderate, 27% severe) line up with Kroenke’s four PHQ-4 bands (0–2, 3–5, 6–8, 9–12), not with the five PHQ-9 bands. Their user mix is much more symptomatic than the 2009 US primary-care sample, in which 61.5% scored 0–2 and only 4.6% scored 9–12.
- A PHQ-2 or GAD-2 of 0–2 is a negative screen on the usual rule; 3–6 is a positive screen and is not itself a severity grade. Severity grading is done on the 0–12 total.

### Gaps
- No European general-population PHQ-4 mean was retrieved, and no Norwegian mean for the Tankevirus age mix.
- No standard minimal important change for the PHQ-4 total, the PHQ-2, or the GAD-2 was found. The 5-point PHQ-9 figure should not be applied to these shorter scales.
- The GAD-2 sensitivity and specificity figures were not re-read in the 2007 Annals paper; they are as quoted by the secondary screening page.

## GAD-7

### Takeaway
The GAD-7 is seven items, scored 0–21. Spitzer’s cuts are 5, 10, and 15, read as mild, moderate, and severe, so the bands are 0–4 minimal, 5–9 mild, 10–14 moderate, and 15–21 severe. A score of 10 or more is the usual screen for generalised anxiety disorder. A 4-point change is the published minimal clinically important difference; a separate reliable-change estimate is 6 points.

### Cited Findings
- Seven items, each 0–3, total 0–21. Higher = worse. Developed in 15 US primary-care clinics, November 2004–June 2005. Of 2740 adults who completed the questionnaire, 965 had a mental-health-professional interview within a week. Mean age 47.4 (SD 15.5); 65% female. — [Spitzer, Kroenke, Williams, and Löwe, 2006, Archives of Internal Medicine](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/410326)
- Citation: Spitzer RL, Kroenke K, Williams JBW, Löwe B. A brief measure for assessing generalized anxiety disorder: the GAD-7. Archives of Internal Medicine. 2006;166(10):1092–1097. DOI: 10.1001/archinte.166.10.1092.
- At a cut of 10 or greater, sensitivity was 89% and specificity 82% for GAD. Most patients with GAD (89%) scored 10 or more; most without GAD (82%) scored under 10. A cut of 15 maximised specificity (95%) but sensitivity fell to 48%. Their Table 1 gives cuts from 8 to 15. The authors wrote that cuts of 5, 10, and 15 “might be interpreted as representing mild, moderate, and severe levels of anxiety on the GAD-7, similar to levels of depression on the PHQ-9.” — [Spitzer et al., 2006](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/410326)
- The 0–4 / 5–9 / 10–14 / 15–21 reading of those cuts is the one used by later scoring summaries of the same paper. — [BYU reliable-change paper quoting Spitzer et al., 2006](https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=5096&context=facpub)
- US primary-care means from Spitzer: in the whole sample of 2739 patients, mean GAD-7 was 6.1 in women and 4.6 in men (SDs for these two means were not in the extracted text). Among interviewed patients, mean (SD) was 14.4 (4.7) in 73 patients with GAD and 4.9 (4.8) in 892 without GAD. Internal consistency Cronbach’s alpha 0.92; test-retest intraclass correlation 0.83. — [Spitzer et al., 2006](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/410326)
- German general population, face-to-face household survey, 5 May–8 June 2006, n=5030, 53.6% female, mean age 48.4 (SD 18.0). Women mean (SD) 3.2 (3.5); men 2.7 (3.2). About 5% scored 10 or greater and 1% scored 15 or greater. Alpha 0.89. — [Löwe et al., 2008, Medical Care, PMID 18388841](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18388841/)
- Citation: Löwe B, et al. Validation and standardization of the Generalized Anxiety Disorder Screener (GAD-7) in the general population. Medical Care. 2008;46(3):266–274. DOI: 10.1097/MLR.0b013e318160d093. The co-author list was not fully captured from the abstract page.
- A German-language report of the same validation gives an overall general-population mean of 2.9 (SD 3.4), n=5036, against a US primary-care mean of 5.2 (SD 5.2) and a diagnosed-GAD mean of 14.3 (SD 4.7), n=73. The sample size (5036 vs 5030) does not match the English paper exactly. — [German-language GAD-7 validation abstract](https://doi.org/10.1055/s-2007-970669)
- Minimal clinically important difference: a 2020 Journal of Affective Disorders paper estimated the MCID at 4 points on the GAD-7 total, in a trial of outpatients with early-onset chronic depression (GAD-7 as a secondary anxiety measure, not an anxiety-treatment trial). The authors said the 4-point estimate needs replication. — [DOI 10.1016/j.jad.2020.01.032](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165032719313643)
- A separate reliable-change analysis, using Jacobson and Truax with a clinical sample and a community sample, put the reliable change index at 5.59, rounded to 6 points. Clinically significant change in that paper required a change of at least 6 points that also crossed the cut of 10. — [BYU GAD-7 reliable-change paper](https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=5096&context=facpub)
- English primary-care psychological-therapy services have used a 4-point GAD-7 change (and a 6-point PHQ-9 change) as the improvement rule. — [Journal of Clinical Epidemiology, 2021, PMC8485844](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8485844/)

### Inferences
- For Mindhelper’s 15–25 age group, a GAD-7 under 5 is the minimal band, 5–9 is mild, and 10 or more is the screen Spitzer recommended. German adults average about 3, inside the minimal band. A score around 14 matches the mean of US primary-care patients who were diagnosed with GAD.
- “Meaningful change” is not settled: 4 points is the MCID estimate and the English service rule; 6 points is the reliable-change figure from the community-versus-clinical analysis. Neither was estimated in adolescents.

### Gaps
- No GAD-7 mean for ages 15–25, and no Danish mean, was retrieved. The German norms are adult household means.
- Full author lists for the 2008 Medical Care paper, the 2020 MCID paper, and the BYU reliable-change paper were not captured.
- Spitzer did not publish standard deviations for the sex-specific means of 6.1 and 4.6.

## CES-D (20-item Center for Epidemiologic Studies Depression Scale)

### Takeaway
The 20-item CES-D runs from 0 to 60. A score of 16 or more is the conventional community screening cut, and Radloff herself called that cut arbitrary. Community means sit around 9. Acutely depressed outpatients in the original clinical samples averaged about 38–39. The published Master Your Mood trial did not use 16 as the floor for inclusion: it enrolled CES-D scores from 10 to 45. A sample mean of 34.5 (SD 8.5) was not found; the 2012 trial mean was 32.3 (SD 8.3).

### Cited Findings
- Twenty items, each 0–3 by frequency in the past week. Range 0–60. Higher = more symptoms. Designed for general-population epidemiology, not as a diagnostic test. Radloff wrote that individual scores should not be interpreted as diagnoses and that “appropriate cutoff scores for clinical screening are yet to be validated.” — [Radloff, 1977, Applied Psychological Measurement](https://conservancy.umn.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/048971e8-d975-4685-8b22-d5f57231a77f/content)
- Citation: Radloff LS. The CES-D Scale: a self-report depression scale for research in the general population. Applied Psychological Measurement. 1977;1(3):385–401. DOI: 10.1177/014662167700100306. Household surveys in Kansas City, Missouri, and Washington County, Maryland, 1971–1974.
- The cut of 16: 70% of 70 Washington County psychiatric inpatients, and 21% of the general-population samples, scored at or above 16. Radloff called 16 an “arbitrary cutoff.” In a separate New Haven group of 35 people admitted for severe depression (Raskin score above 6), the mean CES-D at admission was 39.11 and no score was below 16. — [Radloff, 1977](https://conservancy.umn.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/048971e8-d975-4685-8b22-d5f57231a77f/content)
- Later shorthand treats ≥16 as the screen that “indicates the presence of depressive symptoms and [suggests] the need for further testing.” That is a dictionary summary of the scale, not a new validation. — [APA Dictionary of Psychology](https://dictionary.apa.org/center-for-epidemiologic-studies-depression-scale)
- Diagnostic accuracy, general-population studies: 28 studies, 10,617 participants. At cut 16, sensitivity 0.87 (95% CI 0.82–0.92) and specificity 0.70 (0.65–0.75). Cut 20 had a better trade-off (sensitivity 0.83, specificity 0.78). The authors still describe 16 as the cut “typically recommended.” — [Vilagut et al., 2016, PLoS ONE, PMC4868329](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4868329/)
- Community versus clinical means (US, 1970s). Weissman and colleagues compared the Kansas City and Washington County community surveys with five Connecticut outpatient groups. The paper’s Table 3, as recovered from PDF text, gives a community total mean of 9.10 (SD 8.60, n=3932), with men 7.90 (SD 7.60, n=1614) and women 9.93 (SD 9.14, n=2318). Those three means are internally consistent (the sex-specific means weighted by n equal 9.10). Acutely depressed outpatients, required to score at least 7 on the Raskin scale, had a total mean of 38.10 (SD 9.01, n=148). The methods prose says the community sample numbered 3845, which does not match the table n of 3932. The prose also says people who were not depressed scored “close to the community sample score of 9.” — [Weissman, Sholomskas, Pottenger, Prusoff, and Locke, 1977, American Journal of Epidemiology, 106:203–214](https://anxietyrx.net/docs/Assessing_depressive_symptoms_in_five_psychiatric_populations.pdf)
- Dutch adult cut, as used by the Master Your Mood group: the trial protocol states that “the CES-D cut-off score for clinical depression is 16 (Bouma et al., 1995) for adults, and for adolescents it is either 22 (Cuijpers et al., 2008) or 24 (Roberts et al., 1991).” Bouma is the Dutch CES-D manual. These three cuts are reported here as the protocol quotes them; the Cuijpers 2008 and Roberts 1991 papers were not re-read. — [van der Zanden thesis / protocol PDF, ZonMw](https://www.zonmw.nl/sites/zonmw/files/typo3-migrated-files/Proefschrift_Master_Your_Mood__Grip_Op_Je_Dip_.pdf)
- Master Your Mood (Grip op je Dip) inclusion in the published trial was not 16–45. Inclusion was age 16–25 and a CES-D score between 10 and 45. The protocol says the lower limit of 10 was set by the investigators for “subclinical depression,” because no subclinical cut had been determined. Scores above 45 were outside the enrolled range. Suicidal ideation with intent and plan was an exclusion. — [van der Zanden et al., 2012, Journal of Medical Internet Research](https://www.jmir.org/2012/3/e86); [protocol text in the thesis PDF](https://www.zonmw.nl/sites/zonmw/files/typo3-migrated-files/Proefschrift_Master_Your_Mood__Grip_Op_Je_Dip_.pdf)
- Baseline in that trial, ages 16–25, Netherlands, recruited 2008–2010, n=244: CES-D mean (SD) 32.5 (8.4) in the course arm (n=121) and 32.3 (8.2) in the wait-list arm (n=123); combined 32.3 (8.3). HADS anxiety subscale at the same baseline: 11.2 (3.6) and 11.8 (3.7); combined 11.5 (3.6). — [van der Zanden et al., 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3414873/)
- Change rule used in that trial, not a general CES-D standard: “reliable and clinically significant change” was an improvement of 5 points or more plus a score lower than 22. The cut of 22 is attributed to Cuijpers et al. At 12 weeks, 56% (68/121) of the course arm and 20% (24/123) of the control arm met that rule. — [van der Zanden et al., 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3414873/)
- A later service write-up (Youz, evaluation period 2020–2024, n=118) reports a mean CES-D fall from 34.9 to 26.5 over 6 weeks, and calls a drop of at least 5 points clinically significant. It also says that in 40% of respondents complaints were “resolved,” defined as that 5-point drop plus an end score of “>22” in the functional range. The “>22” wording contradicts the 2012 paper’s functional cut of lower than 22 and is likely a sign error on the web page; it is quoted here as printed. This is a service page, not a journal paper, and it gives no SD. — [Youz, Grip op je Dip evaluation note](https://youz.nl/w/onderzoek-toont-aan-grip-op-je-dip-leidt-tot-vermindering-depressieve-klachten)
- The 20-item scale is the one in all of the figures above. The CES-D-R (revised item set and scoring) and the 10-item short form are different instruments. A cut of 16 does not transfer to them. Their bands were not compiled, because the catalogue use described here is the 20-item 0–60 scale.

### Inferences
- Sixteen is the standard community screen in the sense that Radloff used it, the Dutch adult manual is cited for it, and later reviews call it the typical cut. It is a screen, not a diagnosis. Vilagut’s meta-analysis finds it sensitive and only moderately specific, with 20 performing better as a balanced cut. Dutch adolescent work cited by Master Your Mood uses 22 or 24, not 16, as the clinical cut for adolescents.
- A mean of 32–35, which is where Master Your Mood participants actually sat, is far above the community mean of about 9 and in the same region as treated depression (Weissman acute-depression mean 38.1; Radloff New Haven admission mean 39.1). It is not a borderline screen score.
- The upper exclusion of 45 is a trial/safety gate used by Master Your Mood. It is not a severity band of the scale. The scale continues to 60, and the original depressed samples included scores above 45 (New Haven mean 39 with no score below 16, so the distribution extends well above 45).

### Gaps
- The figure 34.5 (SD 8.5) was not found. The nearest published trial mean is 32.3 (SD 8.3). The nearest later service mean is 34.9, without an SD.
- Radloff’s Table 2 community means (the Kansas City and Washington County household means and SDs) did not survive PDF extraction as numbers. The community mean used here is Weissman’s, from a messy table, cross-checked by weighting the sex-specific cells.
- No single international minimal important change exists. The 5-point rule is the one Master Your Mood used, paired with a score under 22. It should not be quoted as if Radloff or Vilagut had established it.
- Cuijpers et al. 2008 and Roberts et al. 1991 were not opened; the cuts 22 and 24 are only as the Master Your Mood protocol cites them. Bouma et al. 1995 was not opened; the adult cut of 16 is only as that protocol cites the Dutch manual.

## HADS-A (Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale, anxiety subscale)

### Takeaway
HADS-A is seven items, scored 0–21. Zigmond and Snaith’s categories are 0–7 non-case, 8–10 doubtful (often called mild or borderline), and 11 or more a definite case. A score of 8 or more is the cut that most validation studies found gave the best balance of sensitivity and specificity. UK adult means sit around 6. There is no single minimal important change for psychiatric trials.

### Cited Findings
- Fourteen items in total, seven for anxiety and seven for depression. Each item 0–3. Each subscale 0–21. Higher = worse. Built for medical outpatients, with somatic symptoms left out. The two subscales are scored separately. — [Breeman et al., summarising Zigmond and Snaith, Quality of Life Research, DOI 10.1007/s11136-014-0763-z](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11136-014-0763-z)
- Original categories, as restated from Zigmond and Snaith 1983: 0–7 non-case (later called normal), 8–10 doubtful case (later called mild), 11 or more a definite case, on either subscale. Zigmond and Snaith said the cuts needed checking in other settings. — [Breeman et al.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11136-014-0763-z)
- Citation of the original: Zigmond AS, Snaith RP. The Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale. Acta Psychiatrica Scandinavica. 1983;67(6):361–370. DOI: 10.1111/j.1600-0447.1983.tb09716.x. The 1983 PDF retrieved here did not include the numeric cuts; the cuts above are Breeman’s account of that paper.
- A later suggestion, attributed by Breeman to Snaith 2003 (Health and Quality of Life Outcomes, 1:29): 11–14 moderate and 15–21 severe. Breeman notes that no empirical data were presented for this extra cut. — [Breeman et al.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11136-014-0763-z)
- Bjelland review: across studies, the best balance of sensitivity and specificity was usually a score of 8 or above on both subscales. Cronbach’s alpha for HADS-A ranged from 0.68 to 0.93 (mean 0.83). — [Bjelland et al., 2002, Journal of Psychosomatic Research, PMID 11832252](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11832252/)
- Citation: Bjelland I, Dahl AA, Haug TT, Neckelmann D. The validity of the Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale: an updated literature review. Journal of Psychosomatic Research. 2002;52(2):69–77. The author list is the standard citation of PMID 11832252; the PubMed abstract snippet confirmed the ≥8 result and the alpha range, not every co-author name.
- UK non-clinical norms, the set often recommended as UK norms: n=1792 adults, mean age 41.5 (SD 15.9, range 18–91), recruited from workplaces, community centres, and clubs. Anxiety mean 6.14 (SD 3.76), median 6. Depression mean 3.68 (SD 3.07). Distributions were positively skewed. Using Snaith and Zigmond’s 1994 grades, 20.6% of the sample were mild, 10.0% moderate, and 2.6% severe on the anxiety scale. — [Crawford et al., 2001, British Journal of Clinical Psychology, PDF](https://homepages.abdn.ac.uk/j.crawford/pages/dept/pdfs/BJCP_2001_HADS.pdf); [DOI 10.1348/014466501163904](https://doi.org/10.1348/014466501163904)
- Larger UK primary-care-register sample, ages 25–65 (EpiFunD): anxiety scores complete for 6189 people. Median anxiety was 6 (IQR 4–9) in women and 5 (IQR 2–8) in men. The share with anxiety ≥11 (“moderate to severe” in that paper’s labelling of Zigmond and Snaith) was 19.0% in women and 12.5% in men. The authors preferred medians because scores were skewed; a single overall mean was not extracted. — [Breeman et al., DOI 10.1007/s11136-014-0763-z](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11136-014-0763-z)
- Symptomatic youth sample matching Master Your Mood: Dutch 16–25-year-olds with CES-D 10–45, n=244, HADS-A mean (SD) 11.5 (3.6) at baseline. That mean sits on the “definite case” side of the ≥11 cut. The Dutch HADS is described as validated; alpha of the anxiety subscale in the trial was 0.74. — [van der Zanden et al., 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3414873/)
- Minimal important difference is condition-specific, not a psychiatry standard. In idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis after pulmonary rehabilitation, the mean MID estimate for HADS-A was −2 points (range −2.3 to −1.7). — [Edwards et al., 2023, ERJ Open Research](https://openres.ersjournals.com/content/erjor/9/3/00585-2022.full.pdf). In bronchiectasis, anchor-based HADS-A change for patients who felt “a little better” was −2, with other estimates in the same region. — [Wynne et al., PMC7301664](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7301664/). A shoulder-surgery cohort produced HADS-A estimates from 1.4 to 3.8 depending on the method (0.5 SD = 1.7). — [Journal of Clinical Medicine, 2023, PMC9967741](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9967741/)

### Inferences
- For a Master Your Mood write-up, HADS-A of 0–7 is the original non-case range, 8–10 is the doubtful/borderline band, and 11–21 is the definite-case band. Their own baseline mean of 11.5 is just inside the definite-case band, while UK working-age medians are 5–6.
- A 2-point change is a reasonable medical-rehabilitation MID, not a figure established in depressed adolescents. It should be labelled as such if it is used at all.

### Gaps
- No HADS-A mean and SD from an adult outpatient anxiety or depression clinic was retrieved. The clinical-side number above is the Master Your Mood youth sample, who were selected on CES-D rather than on an anxiety diagnosis.
- The original 1983 paper’s own cut table was not in the PDF that was fetched; the cuts are from later authors describing Zigmond and Snaith.
- Bjelland’s exact sensitivity and specificity pair was not copied from the full text. A secondary scale summary quotes 0.90 and 0.78 for HADS-A at ≥8; that pair is not used here because it was not checked against Bjelland’s paper. The PubMed abstract only supports “an optimal balance” at 8 or above.
- No Norwegian or Dutch general-population HADS-A mean was retrieved in this pass. Crawford and Breeman are UK samples.

## EPDS (Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale)

### Takeaway
The EPDS is 10 items, scored 0–30. A cut of 10 or more is the sensitive community screen for possible depression, which is the cut Mamma Mia uses. The original validation cut for probable depression was 12/13, meaning 13 or more. A 2020 individual-patient meta-analysis found that 11 or more maximised combined sensitivity and specificity, and that 13 or more was more specific and less sensitive. A 4-point change is the usual reliable-change figure.

### Cited Findings
- Ten items, each scored 0–3, total 0–30. Higher = worse. Asks about the previous 7 days. Somatic items were left out so that ordinary postnatal physical changes would not inflate the score. Validated in 84 mothers against Research Diagnostic Criteria from Goldberg’s Standardised Psychiatric Interview. — [Cox, Holden, and Sagovsky, 1987, British Journal of Psychiatry, DOI 10.1192/bjp.150.6.782](https://doi.org/10.1192/bjp.150.6.782)
- Citation: Cox JL, Holden JM, Sagovsky R. Detection of postnatal depression: development of the 10-item Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale. British Journal of Psychiatry. 1987;150:782–786.
- Cox’s later account of that validation: a cut of 12/13 (range 0–30) gave sensitivity 86% and specificity 78% for major and minor depression. He also wrote that using 10 or more for the first community screen reduced missed cases, and that 12/13 was the useful cut in a secondary-care parent-and-baby unit. Any score above zero on item 10 (thoughts of self-harm) should be assessed regardless of the total. — [Cox, 2019, British Journal of Psychiatry, DOI 10.1192/bjp.2018.245](https://doi.org/10.1192/bjp.2018.245)
- How the slash notation is used: Gibson and colleagues describe 9/10 as the cut for “possible depression” and 12/13 as the cut for “probable depression,” and say the same pair is used as a marker of possible minor versus major depression. Antenatally, the review presented 14/15 as the probable-depression cut from Murray and Cox. — [Gibson et al., 2009, Acta Psychiatrica Scandinavica](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1600-0447.2009.01363.x)
- In ordinary numbers, 9/10 means a positive screen at 10 or more, and 12/13 means a positive screen at 13 or more. Mamma Mia’s cut of 10 is the possible-depression / community-screen side of that pair, not the original probable-depression cut.
- Individual-participant-data meta-analysis, 58 studies, 15,557 pregnant and postpartum women, 2,069 with major depression. Against a semi-structured interview (36 studies, 9,066 women, 1,330 with major depression): cut ≥10, sensitivity 0.85 (95% CI 0.79–0.90) and specificity 0.84 (0.79–0.88); cut ≥11, sensitivity 0.81 (0.75–0.87) and specificity 0.88 (0.85–0.91); cut ≥13, sensitivity 0.66 (0.58–0.74) and specificity 0.95 (0.92–0.96). Combined sensitivity and specificity were maximised at 11 or higher. Accuracy was similar in pregnant and postpartum women. The authors say 10 or higher and 13 or higher are the cuts most often used, and that 13 or higher can be used when the aim is a higher symptom level. — [Levis et al., 2020, BMJ, 371:m4022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7656313/)
- Citation: Levis B, Negeri Z, Sun Y, Benedetti A, Thombs BD, and the DEPRESSD EPDS Group. Accuracy of the Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale (EPDS) for screening to detect major depression among pregnant and postpartum women: systematic review and meta-analysis of individual participant data. BMJ. 2020;371:m4022. DOI: 10.1136/bmj.m4022. The group authorship is abbreviated here to the named first authors on the PubMed record of that paper.
- UK community perinatal means (ALSPAC): mean EPDS 6.72 (SD 4.94) at 32 weeks of pregnancy and 5.25 (SD 4.61) at 8 months postpartum, among women who completed the questionnaires. The paper used a score above 12 as probable depressive disorder: 13.5% were above that threshold at 32 weeks and 9.1% at 8 weeks postpartum. — [Evans, Heron, Francomb, Oke, and Golding, 2001, BMJ, 323:257](https://www.bmj.com/content/323/7307/257.full)
- A smaller UK general-practice postnatal sample: mean 7 (SD 4.9, range 0–23), n=176; 30/176 (17%) scored in the group the authors treated as depressed at a threshold around 12. — [PMID 9624748](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9624748/)
- Reliable change: Matthey calculated the Jacobson and Truax reliable change index for the EPDS as 4 points. A difference of 4 points is the change needed to be 95% confident it is not measurement error. He illustrated it with the 12/13 major-depression cut for English-speaking women. — [Matthey, 2004, Journal of Affective Disorders, PMID 15013253](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15013253/)
- Citation: Matthey S. Calculating clinically significant change in postnatal depression studies using the Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale. Journal of Affective Disorders. 2004;78(3):269–272. DOI: 10.1016/S0165-0327(02)00313-0.
- A later pregnancy-intervention study (n=117 with pre-post scores) recommended an MCID of 4 points for improvement and 3 points for worsening, averaging distribution-based and anchor-based estimates. — [PMID 34624746](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34624746/)

### Inferences
- Mamma Mia’s cut of 10 is a recognised possible-depression screen. It will flag more women than the original probable-depression cut of 13 or more. Levis’s data say the sensitive and the specific cuts answer different questions: ≥10 (or ≥11) if the aim is not to miss major depression, ≥13 if the aim is a higher-probability case.
- Community perinatal means of about 5–7 sit well below 10. A score of 13 or more is the band Cox validated against major and minor depression, and it is uncommon in the ALSPAC postnatal figures (about 9% at 8 weeks).
- A 4-point change is the figure to use if a single reliable-change number is needed. It is not a severity band.

### Gaps
- No mean and SD for women with a diagnosed postnatal depressive episode was retrieved, and no Norwegian perinatal norm (Mamma Mia’s country) was retrieved. The means above are UK community and antenatal samples, most of whom were not depressed.
- Author list and exact threshold wording for PMID 9624748 were taken from a short PubMed extract (the extract’s punctuation around “<12” and “.12” is garbled; the mean 7, SD 4.9, n=176, and 17% above the depressed threshold are clear).
- Antenatal cuts of 13 and 15, mentioned in a secondary policy paper as Murray and Cox 1990, were not checked in that 1990 paper. Gibson’s review is the source used here for the 14/15 antenatal figure.

## Inventory of Depressive Symptomatology (IDS), as used by Oehler et al. 2020

### Takeaway
Oehler et al. 2020 used the self-rated Inventory of Depressive Symptomatology, range 0–84. That is the 30-item IDS-SR, not the clinician IDS-C and not the 16-item QIDS. The German validation they cite is Drieling, Schärer, and Langosch 2007, not Grässlin 2004. Their baseline means were about 28, which falls at the low end of the moderate band on the IDS-SR30 grades below. QIDS-SR16 bands are listed separately so the two scales are not mixed.

### Cited Findings
- The trial’s primary outcome is named “Inventory of Depressive Symptomatology–self-rating” and “IDS-SR,” with range 0–84. Inclusion was a MINI diagnosis of mild or moderate depressive episode or dysthymia, plus PHQ-9 5–14, age 18 or over, recruited online in Germany. — [Oehler et al., 2020, Journal of Medical Internet Research, 22(7):e15361](https://www.jmir.org/2020/7/e15361/)
- Citation: Oehler C, Görges F, Rogalla M, Rummel-Kluge C, Hegerl U. Efficacy of a guided web-based self-management intervention for depression or dysthymia: randomized controlled trial with a 12-month follow-up using an active control condition. Journal of Medical Internet Research. 2020;22(7):e15361. DOI: 10.2196/15361. Author order is as indexed for that DOI; the page text confirms the outcome and the sample, and the conflicts note lists the initials UH, CR, MR, FG, and CO.
- Baseline IDS-SR mean (SD): iFightDepression arm 27.5 (8.9), n=173; progressive-muscle-relaxation control 27.9 (8.8), n=174. The same table gives baseline PHQ-9 of 9.1 (3.6) and 9.7 (3.3). Cronbach’s alpha of the IDS-SR at baseline was 0.78. — [Oehler et al., 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7388040/)
- The paper’s reference for the German IDS is Drieling T, Schärer LO, Langosch JM. The inventory of depressive symptomatology: German translation and psychometric validation. International Journal of Methods in Psychiatric Research. 2007;16(4):230–236. DOI: 10.1002/mpr.226. Grässlin 2004 is not in the reference list that was extracted. — [Oehler et al., 2020, reference 27](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7388040/)
- What the 0–84 score is: the 30-item IDS, clinician-rated (IDS-C) or self-report (IDS-SR). Respondents rate 30 items, but only 28 are summed, because appetite/weight increase and appetite/weight decrease are alternatives. Each scored item is 0–3. Total 0–84. Higher = worse. The 1996 paper reports this for adult outpatients with current major depression and for euthymic subjects. — [Rush et al., 1996, Psychological Medicine, DOI 10.1017/s0033291700035558](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0033291700035558)
- Citation: Rush AJ, Gullion CM, Basco MR, Jarrett RB, Trivedi MH. The Inventory of Depressive Symptomatology (IDS): psychometric properties. Psychological Medicine. 1996;26(3):477–486. An earlier preliminary paper is Rush et al., Psychiatry Research, 1986 (PMID 3737788); that is not the 1996 psychometric paper.
- IDS-SR30 severity grades as applied by a Spanish primary-care validation, which also states that an IDS-SR score of 18 or above indicates clinically relevant depressive symptoms: none 0–13, mild 14–25, moderate 26–38, severe 39–48, very severe “>49”. In that depressed sample the mean IDS-SR30 at first assessment was 34.8 (SD 15); 12.5% were in 0–13, 22.4% in 14–25, 26.1% in 26–38, 21.8% in 39–48, and 17.2% in the very-severe row. — [Gili et al., 2011, BMC Medical Research Methodology, PMC3188477](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3188477/)
- Citation: Gili M, Luciano JV, Bauzá N, Aguado J, Serrano-Ripoll MJ, Armengol S, Roca M. Psychometric properties of the IDS-SR30 for the assessment of depressive symptoms in Spanish population. BMC Medical Research Methodology. 2011;11:131. The “>49” wording is the table’s, so a score of exactly 49 is not clearly placed.
- The official IDS/QIDS site describes a conversion between IDS-SR, QIDS-SR, and Hamilton totals, adapted from Rush et al. 2003 and Trivedi et al. 2004, with severity coded 0 none through 4 very severe. The numeric cells of that table did not render in the fetch, so the band edges above are Gili’s, not a direct copy of the website. — [ids-qids.org interpretation page](http://ids-qids.org/interpretation.html)
- QIDS-SR16, labelled separately so it is not confused with the IDS-SR30. Sixteen items are collapsed to nine DSM symptom domains; the highest sleep item, the highest appetite/weight item, and the highest psychomotor item are the ones that count. Total 0–27. Scoring instructions: 0–5 no depression, 6–10 mild, 11–15 moderate, 16–20 severe, 21 or more very severe. A QIDS-SR16 of 5 corresponds to a 17-item Hamilton score of about 7 (a common remission threshold). — [CADTH appendix quoting the QIDS scoring instructions and Rush et al. 2003](https://ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK409740/); [STAR*D psychometric report, PMC2929841](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2929841/)
- Citation for the QIDS bands’ source paper, as named by those reviews: Rush AJ, Trivedi MH, Ibrahim HM, Carmody TJ, Arnow B, Klein DN, et al. The 16-item Quick Inventory of Depressive Symptomatology (QIDS), clinician rating (QIDS-C), and self-report (QIDS-SR): a psychometric evaluation in patients with chronic major depression. Biological Psychiatry. 2003;54(5):573–583.
- No minimal clinically important difference has been specified for the IDS-SR or the QIDS-SR. — [CADTH appendix](https://ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK409740/)
- Oehler’s sample-size calculation assumed a 4.0-point between-group difference on the IDS-SR (scaled down from a 5.3-point clinician-rated IDS difference in an earlier face-to-face trial). That 4-point figure is a power assumption, not an MCID. — [Oehler et al., 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7388040/)

### Inferences
- The catalogue’s “Inventar of depressive Symptoms (Rush et al., 1996)” matches the IDS-SR30 that Oehler actually analysed: self-report, 0–84. It does not match the QIDS-SR16 (0–27). A score of 27.5 on the IDS-SR is moderate on Gili’s bands (moderate starts at 26). The same number on the QIDS-SR16 would be the top of the very-severe band. The scale identity decides the interpretation.
- Grässlin 2004 may still be a German edition someone in the catalogue has handled. It is not the edition Oehler’s paper cites. The form (self-report, 0–84) does not depend on that citation.
- Rush’s 1996 outpatient means were not extracted, so the clinical comparison used here is Gili’s Spanish depressed-patient mean of 34.8 (SD 15), which is mid-moderate. Oehler’s baseline of about 28 is lower, as expected in a mild-to-moderate PHQ-9 5–14 sample.

### Gaps
- The exact IDS-SR30 band edges on ids-qids.org were not recovered as numbers. Gili’s table is the numeric source. If the website places “very severe” at 49–84 rather than “>49,” the two differ only at the single score of 49.
- Rush 1996 means and SDs for the 337 adults who completed the 30-item scales were not extracted. Drieling 2007 German norms were not extracted either.
- No European general-population IDS-SR mean was found. The IDS is mainly a clinical scale; a community “normal” band is the 0–13 grade, not a population mean.
- QIDS means are omitted on purpose except for the band edges, because Oehler did not use the QIDS.

## Short Mood and Feelings Questionnaire (S-MFQ / SMFQ), child and adolescent self-report

### Takeaway
The child self-report SMFQ has 13 items, each 0, 1, or 2, total 0–26. Higher = worse. Angold’s original cut for a significant score is 8 or more, with sensitivity 60% and specificity 85% for major depression in the development sample. Later studies, including European and help-seeking adolescent samples, recommend higher cuts, often around 11 or 12, and some recommend different cuts for girls and boys. There is no standard minimal important change.

### Cited Findings
- Thirteen items. Not true = 0, sometimes = 1, true = 2. Total 0–26. The form asks about the past two weeks. A total of 8 or more on the child version “is considered significant,” with sensitivity 60% and specificity 85% for major depression. The scoring sheet says the SMFQ should not be used to make a diagnosis. — [UCSF SMFQ scoring sheet](https://capp.ucsf.edu/sites/g/files/tkssra6871/f/SMFQ%20Scoring.pdf); identical statement on the [Seattle Children’s scoring sheet](https://www.seattlechildrens.org/globalassets/documents/healthcare-professionals/pal/ratings/smfq-rating-scale.pdf)
- The source those sheets name: Angold A, Costello EJ, Messer SC. Development of a short questionnaire for use in epidemiological studies of depression in children and adolescents. International Journal of Methods in Psychiatric Research. 1995;5:237–249. The 1995 paper itself was not retrieved. Some later papers also list Pickles, Winder, and Silver as co-authors; that fuller byline was not checked against the 1995 PDF.
- The parent version exists and is scored the same way. The sheets say sensitivity and specificity for the parent version were not reported in that literature. Cool Minds, as described in the assignment, is a child/adolescent self-report use, so the child form is the relevant one.
- Community adolescents (Australia: Victoria and Western Australia), not a European sample: mean SMFQ at baseline 6.9 (SD 5.5); 22.0% met the caseness rule the authors were using. They defined caseness as 11 or greater, following Patton et al. 2008. A score of 7 or greater was the best predictor of still being a case a year later (sensitivity 76.5%, specificity 67.7%), which is a prognostic cut, not a diagnostic one. The paper describes the items as referring to the past month, which does not match the “past two weeks” wording on the Seattle form. — [McKenzie et al., PMC3734932](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3734932/)
- Help-seeking adolescents, New Zealand, mean age 15.6 (SD 1.59), n=186: mean SMFQ 12.1 (range 0–25, SD 6.0). Against a Children’s Depression Rating Scale–Revised score of 44 or more, the cut the authors preferred was ≥12 (sensitivity 84.2%, specificity 68.2%). The same paper lists published cuts of ≥4 (Rhew et al., 2010), ≥6 (Katon et al., 2008), ≥8 (Angold et al., 1995; Thapar and McGuffin, 1998), ≥10 (Kuo et al., 2005), and ≥11 (Patton et al., 2008; Turner et al., 2014). — [New Zealand validation, PMC6877137](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6877137/)
- Why the cuts differ: in US public middle-school students, Rhew and colleagues’ most acceptable child-SMFQ cut was 4, with specificity only 0.61. They contrast that with Angold’s cut of 8 (sensitivity 0.60, specificity 0.85), obtained in paediatric clinics, ages 6–11, with a one-year diagnostic window. — [Rhew et al., 2010, Child and Adolescent Psychiatry and Mental Health](https://ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2829504/)
- Swedish child and adolescent psychiatry outpatients, the closest European clinical sample retrieved: unselected new outpatients, ages 6–17, n=186 with a diagnostic assessment (Jarbin, Ivarsson, Andersson, Bergman, and Skarphedinsson, PLoS ONE, 2020;15(3):e0230623). The SMFQ did not usefully detect depression under age 13. For adolescents, the authors’ suggested clinical cuts, set to keep sensitivity around 75% or better, were ≥12 for girls and ≥6 for boys. The cut that maximised kappa was ≥17 for girls (sensitivity 0.64, specificity 0.96) and ≥6 for boys (sensitivity 0.93, specificity 0.57). — [Jarbin et al., 2020, PLoS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0230623)
- No widely used minimal important change or reliable-change index for the SMFQ was found in these sources.

### Inferences
- The number to treat as Angold’s own recommendation is 8 or more on the 13-item child form, range 0–26. A community mean around 7 sits just under that cut. A help-seeking adolescent mean around 12 sits above it.
- For Cool Minds, a Danish child and adolescent service, the Swedish outpatient result is the relevant caution: one cut does not fit girls and boys, or children and adolescents. Using 8 for everyone will not match Jarbin’s adolescent-girl cut of 12 or the boy cut of 6. The conflict should be reported rather than collapsed to a single “clinical range.”
- The Australian caseness rule of 11 and the New Zealand help-seeking cut of 12 are closer to each other than either is to Angold’s 8. Angold’s sample was younger (6–11) and clinic-based.

### Gaps
- No Danish community or clinical SMFQ mean was found.
- Means and SDs from Jarbin’s Swedish tables were not extracted (the tables did not come through as text). Only the cuts were.
- The 1995 Angold paper was not opened. Sensitivity 60% and specificity 85% at a cut of 8 are as quoted by the scoring sheets and by Rhew et al.
- No minimal important change was found. Nothing analogous to the PHQ-9’s 5 points should be invented for the SMFQ.

## DASS-21 (Depression Anxiety Stress Scales, 21 items)

### Takeaway
Severity labels for the DASS-21 are applied to subscale scores after they have been doubled, so that they match the 0–42 range of the full DASS. They are not applied to the raw 0–21 totals. On the doubled depression subscale, 0–9 is normal and 28 or more is extremely severe. UK adult doubled means are about 5.7 for depression and 3.8 for anxiety, inside the normal band. A major-depression outpatient mean on the doubled depression subscale was about 30, in the extremely severe band. The labels are population descriptors, not diagnoses.

### Cited Findings
- The DASS-21 has 21 items, seven for depression, seven for anxiety, and seven for stress. Each item is scored 0–3. A raw subscale total therefore runs from 0 to 21. The University of New South Wales scoring instruction is to multiply each DASS-21 subscale total by 2 before comparing it with the DASS normative data or with other published DASS data. The same instruction is printed on the scoring template. — [UNSW DASS FAQ, items 8 and 30](https://dass.psy.unsw.edu.au/DASSFAQ.htm); [DASS scoring template](https://www2.psy.unsw.edu.au/groups/DASS/Download%20files/Dass_template.pdf)
- The severity labels live in the manual and are explicitly for the scores on the same metric as the 42-item DASS. The FAQ says the labels describe where a score sits in the population, and that “mild” means above the population mean but still well below the severity typical of people seeking help. It is not a mild disorder. The authors discourage splitting samples into normal versus clinical for research. — [UNSW DASS FAQ](https://dass.psy.unsw.edu.au/DASSFAQ.htm)
- Manual citation, as given on the forms that reprint the table: Lovibond SH, Lovibond PF. Manual for the Depression Anxiety Stress Scales. 2nd ed. Sydney: Psychology Foundation; 1995. The manual PDF was not opened. The band edges below are from reproductions that cite that manual.
- Bands on the doubled (0–42) metric, from a Queensland government DASS-21 form and from a 2024 Nature Scientific Reports table that reprints the same cuts:
  - Depression: normal 0–9, mild 10–13, moderate 14–20, severe 21–27, extremely severe 28+.
  - Anxiety: normal 0–7, mild 8–9, moderate 10–14, severe 15–19, extremely severe 20+.
  - Stress: normal 0–14, mild 15–18, moderate 19–25, severe 26–33, extremely severe 34+.
  — [MAIC DASS-21 form](https://maic.qld.gov.au/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/DASS-21.pdf); [Nature Scientific Reports table](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-53885-5/tables/1)
- Conflict on one cell only: a clinic handout that also cites Lovibond and Lovibond 1995 prints stress “extremely severe” as 37+ rather than 34+. Depression and anxiety bands on that handout match the table above. The 34+ figure is the one in the government form and the Nature table. — [signaturepsychology DASS-21 sheet](https://signaturepsychology.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/DASS-21_psychometrics.pdf)
- If someone forgets to double: the same labels must not be read against a raw 0–21 total. A raw depression score of 10 is a doubled score of 20, which is the top of the moderate band (14–20), not the mild band (10–13).
- UK general-adult norms for the DASS-21 itself, n=1794, mean age 41.0 (SD 15.9), range 18–91. Henry and Crawford’s Table 1, column order median / mean / SD / range:
  - Raw DASS-21: depression median 1, mean 2.83, SD 3.87, range 0–21; anxiety median 1, mean 1.88, SD 2.95, range 0–20; stress median 4, mean 4.73, SD 4.20, range 0–21.
  - DASS-21 multiplied by 2: depression median 2, mean 5.66, SD 7.74, range 0–42; anxiety median 2, mean 3.76, SD 5.90, range 0–40; stress median 8, mean 9.46, SD 8.40, range 0–42.
  Scores were positively skewed. The doubled depression mean of 5.66 sits inside the normal band (0–9). Alpha: depression 0.88, anxiety 0.82, stress 0.90. — [Henry and Crawford, 2005, British Journal of Clinical Psychology, PDF](https://homepages.abdn.ac.uk/j.crawford/pages/dept/pdfs/BJCP_2005_DASS21.pdf)
- Citation: Henry JD, Crawford JR. The short-form version of the Depression Anxiety Stress Scales (DASS-21): construct validity and normative data in a large non-clinical sample. British Journal of Clinical Psychology. 2005;44(2):227–239. DOI: 10.1348/014466505X29657. PMID: 16004657.
- Clinical means, DASS-21 totals doubled so they match the 42-item scale. Outpatients with major depressive disorder (n=46): depression subscale mean 29.96, anxiety subscale mean 14.04. ### Inferences
- For Bene in Mente, an Italian university-student use, a reported depression score should be checked for whether it was doubled. A doubled score under 10 is the manual’s normal band and is where UK adults sit (mean about 5.7). A doubled score of 14–20 is moderate, 21–27 severe, and 28 or more extremely severe, which is where Antony’s major-depression outpatients sat.
- A raw mean near 2.8 (Henry and Crawford’s undoubled depression mean) is the same information as a doubled mean near 5.7. Comparing a raw score with the 0–9 normal band would make a normal score look clinical.

- No Italian university-student DASS-21 means, and no European student means, were retrieved. Henry and Crawford are UK adults, not students. Antony’s non-clinical group (n=49) is too small and too selected to use as a student norm.
