# BCFPI raw-score gaps for the youth survey

Notes closing the gap left by the companion file `bcfpi.md`. Retrieved 29 October 2026. The survey metric stays as given: never = 0, sometimes = 1, often = 2; six-item sums 0–12; functioning domains 4 items, 0–8. No T-score of 50 or 70 was converted into a raw score. No number below was calculated from a dataset.

## Do the 2009 JCPP papers print raw subscale means?

### Takeaway
Neither 2009 Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry paper is open access, and neither abstract prints a raw subscale mean or SD. No appendix or means table from either paper was retrievable. They do not supply the missing adolescent community raw mean.

### Cited Findings
- Development paper: Cunningham, Boyle, Hong, Pettingill, and Bohaychuk, Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry, 2009, volume 50, issue 4, pages 416–423. It is a confirmatory-factor paper on parent-reported ADHD, oppositional defiant, conduct, separation anxiety, generalized anxiety, and major depression scales. Samples named in the abstract: Ontario Child Health Study–Revised general population n = 1,712 and clinic-referred n = 1,512, plus a provincial implementation sample of clinic-referred 6- to 18-year-olds, n = 56,825. Factor structure was equivalent for boys versus girls and for ages 6–12 versus 13–18. The abstract reports fit indices, not means. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-7610.2008.01970.x); [PubMed 19017368](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19017368/)
- Unpaywall records that development paper as closed: `oa_status` closed, `is_oa` false, `has_repository_copy` false, no open-access location. — [Unpaywall](https://api.unpaywall.org/v2/10.1111/j.1469-7610.2008.01970.x?email=research@evalsum.local)
- Screening paper: Boyle, Cunningham, Georgiades, Cullen, Racine, and Pettingill, same journal, 2009, volume 50, issue 4, pages 424–431. Mother report on 399 outpatient children and adolescents aged 5–17 in three Ontario cities. Abstract results are kappa and ROC area-under-the-curve values (above .80 for conduct, oppositional defiant, ADHD, and separation anxiety; .76 for generalized anxiety; .75 for major depression). AUCs did not differ significantly for boys versus girls or for ages 5–11 versus 12–17. The abstract does not give a T cut, a raw cut, or a subscale mean. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-7610.2008.01971.x); [PubMed 19175807](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19175807/); [Wiley landing page](https://acamh.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1469-7610.2008.01971.x)
- Semantic Scholar marks the screening paper’s open-access PDF status as CLOSED. Unpaywall likewise records no repository copy. — [Semantic Scholar record](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1111/j.1469-7610.2008.01971.x?fields=title,openAccessPdf,externalIds); [Unpaywall](https://api.unpaywall.org/v2/10.1111/j.1469-7610.2008.01971.x?email=research@evalsum.local)

### Inferences
- Equivalence of factor structure across ages 6–12 and 13–18 is not a statement that raw means are equal in those bands. The development abstract does not print those means.
- The screening sample includes adolescents, but it is a clinical mother-report sample, and the published abstract is about classification accuracy, not location of the raw distribution.

### Gaps
- The full texts, including any appendix or table of means, were not open. It remains possible that a paywalled table prints a mean. Nothing in the open record shows that it does, and no raw mean from either paper is cited here.

## What are the exact brief-screen flags, and for which ages?

### Takeaway
On the 2009 youth brief screen, completed by youth aged 12 or over, the printed flags for the four scales are: attention above 7, cooperativeness above 7, managing anxiety above 6, and managing mood above 5. Each line says the score is elevated relative to normed values for 6- to 18-year-olds. The sheet is not split by sex and does not say that the flag equals a T-score of 70.

### Cited Findings
- The form is headed “CHILD & YOUTH MENTAL HEALTH GENERAL SCREENING QUESTIONNAIRE” and “Completed by Youth Aged 12 or Over.” Items are circled never / sometimes / often and scored 0 / 1 / 2. The copyright line on the item page is “BCFPI YOUTH Self Report Brief Screen DRAFT ©BCFPI Inc. 2009.” — [Youth questionnaire](https://www.shared-care.ca/files/Youth_Questionnaire_Final.pdf)
- Quoted flags from the recommendations page of that form. Section 1, regulation of attention, impulsivity and activity: “A score above 7 is considered elevated relative to normed values for 6- to 18-year-olds.” Section 2, oppositional/co-operative behaviour in relationships: “A score above 7 is considered elevated relative to normed values for 6- to 18-year-olds.” Section 5, managing anxiety: “A score above 6 is considered elevated relative to normed values for 6- to 18-year-olds.” Section 6, managing mood: “A score above 5 is considered elevated relative to normed values for 6- to 18-year-olds.” — [Youth questionnaire](https://www.shared-care.ca/files/Youth_Questionnaire_Final.pdf)
- The same recommendations page says the questionnaire is not a diagnostic tool and “should be interpreted by a qualified mental health provider or physician with training in psychometric interpretation.” It does not mention T-scores. — [Youth questionnaire](https://www.shared-care.ca/files/Youth_Questionnaire_Final.pdf)
- The caregiver form uses the same 0/1/2 six-item blocks and the same section rules, including attention above 7 relative to normed values for 6- to 18-year-olds. — [Caregiver questionnaire](https://shared-care.ca/files/Caregiver_Questionnaire_Final.pdf)
- A sum of 7 is not above 7. On a 0–12 attention or cooperativeness sum, the printed line is 8, 9, 10, 11, or 12. Anxiety’s line is above 6. Mood’s line is above 5. These four flags are not the same number.

### Inferences
- The age band printed next to the flag is 6–18, even though the youth form is filled in by youth aged 12 or over. The sheet does not give a separate 12–18 or 13–18 raw flag, and it does not split girls from boys.
- “Above 7” is a screening threshold on the published six-item sum. It is not a community mean, and the sheet does not state which T-score it approximates.

### Gaps
- The sheet does not say whether the youth-form flags were estimated from youth self-report norms or copied from the parent form.

## Is there a printed Ontario adolescent community raw mean on 0–12?

### Takeaway
No open source printed an Ontario community raw mean and SD for adolescents aged 12–18, by sex, on the six-item 0–12 scales. A 2006 thesis does print parent-report community raw means, but the sample is mixed-age (one mean age of 10.96 years for the whole file), not split by sex, and not an adolescent table. Its mood mean is a 9-item sum, not 0–12. Those figures are not the missing adolescent norm.

### Cited Findings
- Bova (2006) analysed 3,265 BCFPI parent reports: 340 First Nations clinical reports, 1,242 non-First Nations clinical norm reports, and 1,683 non-First Nations general-population norm reports. “The average age of the participants was 10.96 years (SD 3.59).” That sentence is for the participants as a whole. The thesis does not print a separate mean age for the community file or for ages 12–18. Respondents used 0 = “never or not true,” 1 = “sometimes true,” 2 = “often or very true.” Official BCFPI output is described as T-scores with mean 50 and SD 10, compared with norms for ages 6–12 or 13–18. — [Bova, 2006, PDF](https://knowledgecommons.lakeheadu.ca/bitstreams/bf84d1ad-40d4-41b4-a69f-644d12b32d0b/download)
- Tables 4 and 5 print raw scale means. Non-First Nations general-population column, parent report, not split by sex or age: regulation of attention, impulsivity, and activity, 6 items, mean 3.20, SD 2.69, range 0–12; cooperativeness, 6 items, mean 3.24, SD 2.53, range 0–12; managing anxiety, 6 items, mean 2.99, SD 2.27, range 0–12; managing mood, 9 items, mean 1.59, SD 2.02, range 0–13. — [Bova, 2006, Tables 4 and 5](https://knowledgecommons.lakeheadu.ca/bitstreams/bf84d1ad-40d4-41b4-a69f-644d12b32d0b/download)
- Same tables, non-First Nations clinical column: attention, 6 items, mean 6.41, SD 3.27, range 0–12; cooperativeness, 6 items, mean 7.11, SD 3.25, range 0–12; managing anxiety, 6 items, mean 4.49, SD 3.14, range 0–12; managing mood, 9 items, mean 5.81, SD 3.77, range 0–17. This is a clinical parent-report mean in a mixed-age norm file, not an adolescent community mean. — [Bova, 2006, Tables 3 and 5](https://knowledgecommons.lakeheadu.ca/bitstreams/bf84d1ad-40d4-41b4-a69f-644d12b32d0b/download)
- The nine mood items in that thesis are the six brief-screen mood items plus “Has lost a lot of weight without trying,” “Talks about killing himself/herself,” and “Deliberately harms self or attempts suicide.” The community mood mean of 1.59 is on that 9-item sum. — [Bova, 2006, Table 1](https://knowledgecommons.lakeheadu.ca/bitstreams/bf84d1ad-40d4-41b4-a69f-644d12b32d0b/download)
- A Swedish outpatient study, not an Ontario community study, prints raw means for parent BCFPI interviews of new CAMHS referrals. Ages 13–17, n = 113, sexes combined in that column: ADHD mean 6.54 (SD 3.07), ODD 6.93 (SD 3.27), GAD 5.27 (SD 3.11), MDD 6.19 (SD 3.35). The paper describes six subscales from 36 questions scored never 0, sometimes 1, often 2, and says results are ordinarily T-scores with norms for boys and girls aged 6–12 and 13–17. Table 1 of that paper reports externalizing and internalizing as T-values, not raw scores. Table 2’s subscale rows are raw means in a clinical sample in which no child had a non-clinical Children’s Global Assessment Scale score. — [Högberg et al., 2018, Tables 1 and 2](https://reference-global.com/download/article/10.21307/sjcapp-2018-009.pdf)
- That Swedish paper’s Table 3 “Cut” column is the raw score used for kappa so that the screen-positive rate matched the prevalence of a Longitudinal Expert All Data diagnosis in that clinic sample. The printed cuts include ADHD 7, ODD 10, GAD 11, and MDD 7. The note says the cut is for the kappa analyses. It is not a community norm and not the 2009 brief-screen rule. The ADHD row is the six-item scale; a separate ADD row is three inattention questions, cut 4, mean about 3.9. — [Högberg et al., 2018, Table 3](https://reference-global.com/download/article/10.21307/sjcapp-2018-009.pdf)
- Ontario’s open “BCFPI new referrals” file, extracted from 2008–2013 annual reports, is described as covering age, gender, and Ministry region. The catalogue text does not list subscale scores, so it is not a printed table of raw means. — [Open Government Portal](https://open.canada.ca/data/en/dataset/19a71ee7-ac61-4c58-9f8a-202f69ba230d)
- Cook et al. (2013) Table 1 means remain T-scores for a mixed-age inpatient sample (mean age 12.06) and an outpatient column from a personal communication. They are not raw 0–12 means. — [Cook et al., 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3647631/)

### Inferences
- The only Ontario community raw means located are Bova’s parent-report figures for a file whose overall mean age is about 11. Attention 3.20 (SD 2.69), cooperativeness 3.24 (SD 2.53), and managing anxiety 2.99 (SD 2.27) are on the six-item 0–12 metric. They are not means for ages 12–18, not youth self-report, and not split by sex. Using them as the adolescent community benchmark would mis-state the sample.
- The community mood figure of 1.59 cannot be placed on the survey’s 0–12 mood sum. It includes three items the six-item mood scale does not have.
- The Swedish 13–17 means are clinical parent-report means in Sweden. They show that raw 0–2 sums are sometimes printed for adolescents, and they still are not an Ontario community norm. The kappa cuts in that paper, including ADHD at 7, are prevalence-matched cuts in a treated sample. They are not evidence for the brief-screen line and not a conversion of T = 70.

### Gaps
- No open source printed the adolescent (12–18) Ontario community raw mean and SD, by sex, for youth self-report or for parent report, on the six-item 0–12 scales. That is the number the survey comparison still lacks.
- Bova does not separate the community raw mean into ages 6–12 versus 13–18, which is the split the manual uses for T-scores. The cell means that would turn a raw sum into T = 70 were not in the thesis tables.
- The 2009 papers’ full texts remain closed, so a hidden means table there was not ruled out by reading the PDF.

## Which six items are published, and which survey item is the mismatch?

### Takeaway
The published six-item lists agree across the 2009 youth brief screen, Cook et al. (2013) Table 4, and Bova (2006) Table 1. Attention matches the survey list. Cooperativeness, managing anxiety, and managing mood each differ by one item: the survey adds “lose temper,” “anxious or nervous,” and “lonely,” and omits “easily annoyed by others,” “overly anxious to please people,” and “trouble enjoying yourself.”

### Cited Findings
- Regulating attention, youth form, six items: easily distracted, have trouble sticking to activities; fail to finish things you start; have difficulty following directions or instructions; impulsive, act without stopping to think; jump from one activity to another; fidget. — [Youth questionnaire](https://www.shared-care.ca/files/Youth_Questionnaire_Final.pdf)
- The same six, parent wording, in Cook et al. Table 4: distractible, has trouble sticking to an activity; jumps from one activity to another; fails to finish things he starts; fidgets; difficulty following directions or instructions; impulsive, acts without stopping to think. Bova Table 1 lists the same six. — [Cook et al., 2013, Table 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3647631/); [Bova, 2006, Table 1](https://knowledgecommons.lakeheadu.ca/bitstreams/bf84d1ad-40d4-41b4-a69f-644d12b32d0b/download)
- The survey attention content already recorded in the companion notes (fail to finish, easily distracted, jump between activities, difficulty following instructions, impulsive, fidget) is that six. No survey item is extra, and no published item is missing. The “above 7” flag is on this item set.
- Cooperativeness, youth form, six items: cranky; defiant, talk back to adults; blame others for your own mistakes; easily annoyed by others; argue a lot with adults; angry and resentful. Cook Table 4 and Bova Table 1 list the same six, with “talks back to adults” and “blames others for own mistakes.” — [Youth questionnaire](https://www.shared-care.ca/files/Youth_Questionnaire_Final.pdf); [Cook et al., 2013, Table 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3647631/); [Bova, 2006, Table 1](https://knowledgecommons.lakeheadu.ca/bitstreams/bf84d1ad-40d4-41b4-a69f-644d12b32d0b/download)
- The survey cooperativeness list already recorded in the companion notes is blame others, irritable/angry, lose temper, defiant, argue with adults, resentful. Extra survey item: lose temper. Published item absent from that list: easily annoyed by others. Cranky versus irritable/angry is a wording difference, not a second extra item. The “above 7” flag includes easily annoyed and cranky, and it does not include lose temper.
- Managing anxiety, youth form, six items: worry about doing better at things; worry about past behaviour; worry about doing the wrong thing; worry about things in the future; afraid of making mistakes; overly anxious to please people. Cook Table 4 and Bova Table 1 list the same six. — [Youth questionnaire](https://www.shared-care.ca/files/Youth_Questionnaire_Final.pdf); [Cook et al., 2013, Table 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3647631/); [Bova, 2006, Table 1](https://knowledgecommons.lakeheadu.ca/bitstreams/bf84d1ad-40d4-41b4-a69f-644d12b32d0b/download)
- The companion notes say the survey managing-anxiety list shares five of those items and, in place of “overly anxious to please people,” has “anxious or nervous.” Extra survey item: anxious or nervous. Published item absent from the survey list: overly anxious to please people. The “above 6” flag is for the published six, including overly anxious to please people.
- Managing mood, youth form, six items: no interest in your usual activities; get no pleasure from your usual activities; trouble enjoying yourself; not as happy as other children; feel hopeless; unhappy, sad, or depressed. Cook Table 4 loads the same six, with the wording “has trouble enjoying self.” — [Youth questionnaire](https://www.shared-care.ca/files/Youth_Questionnaire_Final.pdf); [Cook et al., 2013, Table 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3647631/)
- The companion notes say the survey mood list shares five of those items and substitutes “lonely” for “trouble enjoying yourself.” Extra survey item: lonely. Published item absent from the survey list: trouble enjoying yourself. The “above 5” flag is for the published six. Bova’s ninth, eighth, and seventh mood items (weight loss, talks about killing self, deliberate self-harm or suicide attempt) are also absent from both the brief-screen six and the survey list. — [Bova, 2006, Table 1](https://knowledgecommons.lakeheadu.ca/bitstreams/bf84d1ad-40d4-41b4-a69f-644d12b32d0b/download)

### Inferences
- For attention, the brief-screen flag and Bova’s six-item raw mean describe the same item set the survey sums. The mean is still the mixed-age parent mean, not an adolescent self-report mean.
- For cooperativeness, anxiety, and mood, a one-item swap means the published raw flag and Bova’s raw mean are for a different sum than the survey column. The mismatch is item identity, not a difference in the 0–2 anchors.

### Gaps
- The survey wordings used here are the lists already written in the companion notes, not a fresh export of the survey file. If a survey item is only a paraphrase of the published item, the flag would apply; the companion notes treat lose temper, anxious or nervous, and lonely as substitute items rather than paraphrases.

## Are published functioning scales three items, and is there a raw flag?

### Takeaway
Published functioning subscales are three items each and are reported as T-scores. The survey’s four-item sums on 0–8 are a different score. No raw-score flag for the three-item functioning scales was found. The 2009 brief screen has no functioning section.

### Cited Findings
- Gordon and colleagues, describing Ontario outpatient intake with the BCFPI, define three functioning subscales and report them as T-scores: child’s social participation, a three-item subscale (withdrawing or spending less time with other children); quality of the child’s social relationships, a three-item subscale (poor relationships); school participation and achievement, described in the same source as the third impairment measure. High T-scores indicate more impairment. — [Gordon et al., 2006](https://doi.org/10.1177/1087054705283881)
- Bova (2006), citing the manual: Child’s Social Participation has three items, example “Doing things less with other kids.” Quality of the Child’s Social Relationships has three items, example “Getting along with you and your partner.” School Participation and Achievement has three items, example “Getting along with his/her teachers.” High scores indicate poorer functioning. No raw mean for these scales appears in Bova’s Tables 3–5, which are the mental-health scales. — [Bova, 2006](https://knowledgecommons.lakeheadu.ca/bitstreams/bf84d1ad-40d4-41b4-a69f-644d12b32d0b/download)
- The current case-report guide, step on child functioning: review the Child Functioning scale for impact on social participation, social relationships, and school participation. “Higher T scores reflect higher levels of functional impairment.” The guide’s general rule remains a T-score of 70 as significantly elevated and 65 as a possible borderline score. It does not add a raw-score flag for functioning. — [BCFPI case-report interpretation guide](https://bcfpi.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/bcfpi_interpreting_case_report.pdf)
- The 2009 youth brief screen’s six sections are attention, cooperativeness, conduct, separation, managing anxiety, and managing mood. There is no functioning block and no raw flag analogous to “above 7.” — [Youth questionnaire](https://www.shared-care.ca/files/Youth_Questionnaire_Final.pdf)
- Cook et al. Table 1 prints functioning as T-scores (social participation, quality of relationships, school participation and achievement, global functioning), parent report, mixed ages, no SDs. Those are not 0–8 means and not a raw cut. — [Cook et al., 2013, Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3647631/)

### Inferences
- A survey sum of 0–8 on four items is not the published three-item subscale. Any T = 70 language in the case-report guide is a T-score rule for the published scale, not a raw cut on 0–8, and not a raw cut on the three-item version.
- No 3-item raw-score flag was found to label. The absence is the finding: the open sources that describe the three-item scales describe them as T-scores.

### Gaps
- The exact three wordings inside each published functioning subscale were not in an item table. Only the examples and content glosses above were found.
- No community or clinical raw mean for the three-item functioning scales, and no raw mean for a four-item 0–8 version, was found.
