# Locate catalogue scores on published instruments

Symptom scales named in the EvalSum submissions place general-population adults in the non-case band and place treated groups one or two bands higher. German household means are **3.1** for women and **2.7** for men on the PHQ-9, and **3.2** and **2.7** on the GAD-7; a large US community CES-D mean is **9.1**; a UK adult HADS anxiety mean is **6.14**; UK perinatal EPDS means are **6.72** in late pregnancy and **5.25** eight months after birth; the 2014 German SSS-8 mean is **3.23**; and a Québec general-practice Insomnia Severity Index mean is **8.27** ([Kocalevent, Hinz and Brähler 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.genhosppsych.2013.04.006); [Löwe et al. 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18388841/); [Weissman et al. 1977](https://anxietyrx.net/docs/Assessing_depressive_symptoms_in_five_psychiatric_populations.pdf); [Crawford et al. 2001](https://doi.org/10.1348/014466501163904); [Evans et al. 2001](https://www.bmj.com/content/323/7307/257.full); [Gierk et al. 2014](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/1783305); [Gagnon et al. 2013](https://www.jabfm.org/content/26/6/701)). Clinical comparison means sit far from those floors: **17.1** for interviewed major depression on the PHQ-9, **14.4** for diagnosed generalised anxiety on the GAD-7, **38.1** for acutely depressed outpatients on the CES-D, **27.5–27.9** at baseline in the German iFightDepression trial on the 0–84 self-rated Inventory of Depressive Symptomatology and **34.8** in a Spanish depressed primary-care sample, and **12.96** for German psychosomatic outpatients on the SSS-8 ([Kroenke, Spitzer and Williams 2001](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1495268/); [Spitzer et al. 2006](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/410326); [Weissman et al. 1977](https://anxietyrx.net/docs/Assessing_depressive_symptoms_in_five_psychiatric_populations.pdf); [Oehler et al. 2020](https://www.jmir.org/2020/7/e15361/); [Gili et al. 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3188477/); [Gierk et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpsychores.2014.11.006)). Well-being runs the other way. Adult anchors cluster near **68–70** on the WHO-5 percentage score, **51–52** on the 14-item Warwick-Edinburgh scale, a Rasch metric of **23.6** on its short form, **77.9** on the Norwegian EQ-VAS, and **15.5** on a small Dutch mental-health quality-of-life panel, against depression-range anchors near **28** on the WHO-5, **55.6** on the EQ-VAS, **11.5** on that quality-of-life sum, and **26.5** on the SF-12 mental component ([Topp et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1159/000376585); [Kliem et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1592614); [Health Survey for England 2011 norms](https://warwick.ac.uk/services/innovations/wemwbs/how/wemwbs_population_norms_in_health_survey_for_england_data_2011.pdf); [Garratt et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11136-021-02938-7); [van Krugten et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11136-021-02935-w); [Saragoussi et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.2147/NDT.S159276)). The lookup tables below give the range, the direction, the intervention, and the published anchors. The readings that do not survive contact with the instrument are metric swaps, author-chosen thresholds, and cousin scales standing in for a local form.

## Community symptom means sit in the non-case band

The useful pair for an editor is a community mean and a clinical mean, because the printed “cutoff” on these instruments is a screen, and the screen often sits much closer to the general population than to people in treatment. On the PHQ-9 (0–27, higher worse), Kroenke’s bands are **0–4** none or minimal, **5–9** mild, **10–14** moderate, **15–19** moderately severe, and **20–27** severe, with **≥10** the usual major-depression screen (sensitivity 88% and specificity 88% against an interview in 580 primary-care patients). In that same interview sample the mean was **3.3** with no depressive disorder, **10.4** with another depressive disorder, and **17.1** with major depression. German adults, surveyed in households from 2003 to 2008, already sit in the bottom band: women **3.1** (SD 3.5), men **2.7** (SD 3.5) ([Kroenke et al. 2001](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1495268/); [Kocalevent et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.genhosppsych.2013.04.006)). A catalogue baseline near 9, which is where iFightDepression started (arm means **9.1** and **9.7**, inside an inclusion window of PHQ-9 5–14), is the top of the mild band. It is a long way below the interviewed major-depression mean of 17 ([Oehler et al. 2020](https://www.jmir.org/2020/7/e15361/)).

The same geometry repeats. The GAD-7 (0–21) uses cuts of **5, 10 and 15**, read as mild, moderate and severe, so the bands are **0–4, 5–9, 10–14 and 15–21**, and **≥10** is the usual screen for generalised anxiety (sensitivity 89%, specificity 82%). German adults average **3.2** (women) and **2.7** (men), with about **5%** at or above 10 and about **1%** at or above 15, while US primary-care patients who met criteria for generalised anxiety averaged **14.4** ([Spitzer et al. 2006](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/410326); [Löwe et al. 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18388841/)). The PHQ-4 (0–12), which Tankevirus uses, splits into **0–2** normal, **3–5** mild, **6–8** moderate and **9–12** severe; each two-item half, the PHQ-2 and the GAD-2, screens positive at **3 or more** on a 0–6 subscale. In Kroenke’s US primary-care sample the mean was **2.5**, and **61.5%** scored 0–2 while **4.6%** scored 9–12 ([Kroenke et al. 2009](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0033318209708643)). Tankevirus’s reported mix — **13%** normal, **33%** mild, **27%** moderate, **27%** severe — uses those four bands and describes a far more symptomatic group than that primary-care sample.

On the 20-item CES-D (0–60), the conventional community screen is **16 or more**. Radloff called 16 arbitrary: **21%** of her general-population samples and **70%** of a small inpatient group scored at or above it, and a New Haven group admitted for severe depression averaged **39.11** with no score below 16. Weissman’s community total mean is **9.10** (SD 8.60); acutely depressed outpatients averaged **38.10** (SD 9.01). A later diagnostic meta-analysis still calls 16 the typical cut (sensitivity 0.87, specificity 0.70) and finds **20** a better balance ([Radloff 1977](https://conservancy.umn.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/048971e8-d975-4685-8b22-d5f57231a77f/content); [Weissman et al. 1977](https://anxietyrx.net/docs/Assessing_depressive_symptoms_in_five_psychiatric_populations.pdf); [Vilagut et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4868329/)). HADS anxiety (0–21) is **0–7** non-case, **8–10** doubtful, and **11 or more** a definite case; across validation studies the best balance was usually **8 or above**. UK non-clinical adults averaged **6.14** (SD 3.76). The EPDS (0–30) uses **10 or more** as the sensitive community screen for possible depression and **13 or more** as Cox’s original probable-depression cut (the 12/13 rule). An individual-participant meta-analysis maximised combined sensitivity and specificity at **11 or more**, with **≥13** more specific and less sensitive. ALSPAC community means were **6.72** at 32 weeks of pregnancy and **5.25** at eight months postpartum ([Breeman et al.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11136-014-0763-z); [Bjelland et al. 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11832252/); [Crawford et al. 2001](https://doi.org/10.1348/014466501163904); [Cox 2019](https://doi.org/10.1192/bjp.2018.245); [Levis et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7656313/); [Evans et al. 2001](https://www.bmj.com/content/323/7307/257.full)).

Child, somatic and sleep scales follow the same pattern with narrower margins. The 13-item Short Mood and Feelings Questionnaire runs **0–26**, and Angold’s sheet treats **8 or more** as a significant score (sensitivity 60%, specificity 85% in the development sample). An Australian community adolescent mean was **6.9**; a New Zealand help-seeking adolescent mean was **12.1**, where the preferred cut was **≥12**. Swedish child-and-adolescent psychiatry outpatients did not support one cut: the authors’ clinical suggestions were **≥12 for adolescent girls** and **≥6 for adolescent boys** ([SMFQ scoring sheet](https://www.seattlechildrens.org/globalassets/documents/healthcare-professionals/pal/ratings/smfq-rating-scale.pdf); [McKenzie et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3734932/); [New Zealand validation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6877137/); [Jarbin et al. 2020](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0230623)). The SSS-8 runs **0–32**. Gierk’s German general-population mean was **3.23**, and the percentile bands are minimal **0–3**, low **4–7**, medium **8–11**, high **12–15** and very high **16–32**, with high or very high built to be about the top **5%**. A later German community sample (mean **4.64**) put **5.6%** in high and **4.3%** in very high. Psychosomatic outpatients averaged **12.96**, the floor of the high band ([Gierk et al. 2014](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/1783305); [Gierk et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpsychores.2014.11.006); [German community norms, 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2026.1851921/full)). The Insomnia Severity Index runs **0–28**, with guideline bands **0–7** none, **8–14** subthreshold, **15–21** moderate and **22–28** severe. A community case-finding cut of **10** and a primary-care cut of **14** are empirical alternatives; unselected general-practice attenders averaged **8.27** ([Morin et al. 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3079939/); [Gagnon et al. 2013](https://www.jabfm.org/content/26/6/701)).

## Well-being normals cluster near two-thirds of the scale

Higher is better on the WHO-5, both Warwick-Edinburgh forms, the EQ-VAS, the MHQoL, the SF-12 component scores, the Subjective Happiness Scale and the Valued Living Questionnaire, so the clinical-looking score is the low one. The WHO-5 has a raw total of **0–25** and a percentage score of raw × 4, range **0–100**. A percentage at or below **50** (raw **12 or below**; raw 13 × 4 = 52) is the screening cut for poor well-being and possible depression. Topp treats a percentage around **28 or below** as major-depression level, and cites a Danish general-population mean of **70**. German adults in 2021 averaged raw **16.89** (SD 5.74), which is a percentage mean of **67.6**; a percentage of 48 covered about the lowest fifth of adults (21.3%) and 15.6% of ages 16–24, and a percentage of 28 covered 9.5% of adults. A **10-point** change on the 0–100 scale is the working threshold Topp cites for a clinically relevant change. A 2025 review says that figure is a 2.5-point raw difference judged relevant in antidepressant trial data, and that a formal minimal important difference has not been established ([WHO-5 scoring sheet](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/mental-health/who-5_english-original4da539d6ed4b49389e3afe47cda2326a.pdf); [Topp et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1159/000376585); [Kliem et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1592614); [Advances in Therapy 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12325-025-03266-9)). Mindhelper and Nebolus should be read on the percentage scale against that 68–70 adult anchor. No Danish youth mean and no German pupil mean were retrieved.

The 14-item Warwick-Edinburgh Mental Wellbeing Scale runs **14–70**. Health Survey for England 2011, ages 16 and over, averaged **51.61** (SD 8.71, median 53). The low-well-being band used on English data is the bottom 15%, scores **14–42**, and the high band is **60–70**. Danish adults averaged **52.2** (SD 8.7). In the same four-country comparison Iceland ranked between Denmark and England, and the Icelandic mean itself was not in the retrieved text, so HappApp has a plausible low-50s adult neighbourhood and no published Icelandic number to quote. The scale was built for population monitoring. Individual change estimates span **3 to 8** points. No depression-sample mean for the 14-item form was retrieved ([Warwick scoring guidance](https://warwick.ac.uk/services/innovations/wemwbs/how/); [HSE 2011 norms](https://warwick.ac.uk/services/innovations/wemwbs/how/wemwbs_population_norms_in_health_survey_for_england_data_2011.pdf); [Koushede et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.psychres.2018.12.003)). The 7-item short form is a different score. Raw answers still sum to **7–35**, and UK norms are **Rasch-transformed metric** scores: Health Survey for England 2011 mean **23.61** (SD 3.90). Low well-being is metric **7.0–19.5**. English ages 16–24 sit almost on the adult metric mean (women **23.17**, men **23.57**). A raw sum in the mid-20s placed next to 23.6 looks high and can still be ordinary. Mindhelper’s short-form score is comparable with 23.6 only after the conversion ([Ng Fat et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11136-016-1454-8); [Warwick scoring guidance](https://warwick.ac.uk/services/innovations/wemwbs/how/)).

Health-related scores split the same way once the scale is identified. The EQ-VAS is overall health today, **0–100**, and is not the index used for QALYs. Norway’s weighted adult mean is **77.9** (SD 18.3), which is the figure behind Tankevirus’s statement that the population norm is 78. Adults on or at risk of sick leave for depression or anxiety averaged **55.6** (SD 17.7) on the VAS and **0.631** on the EQ-5D index (UK crosswalk). The paired population index in the same comparison is **0.805**, also on the UK crosswalk, because Norway had no 5L value set at the time of that norm. A baseline VAS of **57** matches the Norwegian depression-and-anxiety mean, and a QALY calculation belongs on the index near 0.81 and 0.63, not on the VAS of 78 ([Garratt et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11136-021-02938-7); [Sandin et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.655151)). The MHQoL-7D sums seven items to **0–21**, with a separate 0–10 psychological well-being VAS. The only Dutch general-population sum retrieved is **15.5** (SD 2.9, N = 110, mean age 48.6) against **11.5** (SD 4.0) in mental-healthcare users. GetStarted’s students have no published student norm. A seven-country COVID-period public mean was **14.1**, and the lowest young-adult cell retrieved was **12.3** among UK adults aged 18–24 ([van Krugten et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11136-021-02935-w); [Enzing et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12955-022-02041-6)).

The SF-12 mental and physical component summaries are normed so that a US general-population score has mean **50** and SD **10**. European adults in the ESEMeD survey averaged **53.7** on the mental component, with Germany near **54.4**. The best screening cut for a current DSM-IV depressive disorder in that survey was **45.6**. That cut is a population screen. Outpatients starting or switching an antidepressant in five European countries, including Germany, averaged **26.5** (SD 9.2) at baseline and **38.7** twelve months later, still below the impairment line of **42** that those authors cite. Their physical component stayed near **45**. For iFightDepression, a mental-component score in the mid-20s is a treated-depression mean, a score under 42 is the impairment threshold those authors use, and a score under 45.6 is a sensitive screen that many people without a diagnosis will cross ([Vilagut et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jval.2013.01.006); [Saragoussi et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.2147/NDT.S159276)). The Subjective Happiness Scale is a **1–7** item mean (a sum of 4–28 is that mean times 4). US college samples fell around **4.6–5.1**; one US community-adult mean was **5.62**. The original paper defines no clinical cut. An Italian community norming study exists, and its mean was not in the retrieved text, so Bene in Mente has a college cluster to cite and no Italian number ([Lyubomirsky and Lepper 1999](https://doi.org/10.1023/a:1006824100041)). The Valued Living Questionnaire composite, the mean of ten importance × consistency products, ran about **60–64** in undergraduate samples, on a composite that the manual places from **10 to 100**. There is no clinical cut, and a 12-domain score divided by 12 is a different formula ([Wilson et al. manuscript](https://contextualscience.org/sites/default/files/VLQ%20manuscript%20Wilson%20Sandoz%20et%20al.pdf); [VLQ working manual](https://contextualscience.org/files/VLQ%20working%20manual%20v1-15-08.pdf)).

## Submission rules miss the published instrument

Several catalogue rules are real study decisions that answer a narrower question than “this score is clinical.” Master Your Mood enrolled Dutch 16–25-year-olds with a CES-D **between 10 and 45**, and the protocol says 10 was an investigator choice for “subclinical” depression because no subclinical cut had been set. The published baseline was **32.3** (SD 8.3) overall, with arm means 32.5 and 32.3. That is in the same region as Weissman’s acute-depression mean of 38 and Radloff’s admission mean of 39. It is not a borderline screen of 16. The trial’s own “reliable and clinically significant change” was an improvement of **5 points or more** plus a score **lower than 22**, and the protocol quotes adolescent clinical cuts of **22** (Cuijpers et al. 2008) or **24** (Roberts et al. 1991) rather than the adult 16. A later service page reports a mean fall from **34.9** to **26.5** and then defines resolution with an end score of “>22,” which reverses the 2012 functional cut. The figure **34.5 (SD 8.5)** was not found. The 5-point rule is the trial’s rule. The upper gate of 45 is a safety exclusion, and the scale continues to 60 ([van der Zanden et al. 2012](https://www.jmir.org/2012/3/e86); [protocol PDF](https://www.zonmw.nl/sites/zonmw/files/typo3-migrated-files/Proefschrift_Master_Your_Mood__Grip_Op_Je_Dip_.pdf); [Youz evaluation note](https://youz.nl/w/onderzoek-toont-aan-grip-op-je-dip-leidt-tot-vermindering-depressieve-klachten)). The same trial’s HADS anxiety baseline was **11.5** (SD 3.6), just inside the definite-case band, against a UK adult mean of 6.14. A 2-point HADS change appears in lung and shoulder rehabilitation studies and was not established in depressed adolescents ([van der Zanden et al. 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3414873/); [Crawford et al. 2001](https://doi.org/10.1348/014466501163904)).

I-Sleep’s improvement rule — an ISI drop of **at least 8**, or a score **below 11** — tracks Morin’s moderate-improvement anchor (mean change **−8.4** among people rated moderately improved; an ROC cut of a reduction greater than 7 separated moderate or marked improvement from slight) and Morin’s suggestion that **11** is the compromise for counting someone as a case in a clinical trial. A score below 11 can still sit in the subthreshold band of 8–10. The original no-insomnia band is **0–7**. A primary-care screening cut of **14** is a third, looser line: 11, 12 and 13 are below 14 and are not below 11 ([Morin et al. 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3079939/); [Gagnon et al. 2013](https://www.jabfm.org/content/26/6/701)). Mamma Mia’s EPDS cut of **10** is the recognised possible-depression community screen. Cox’s probable-depression cut, and the cut that flags a higher-probability case in Levis’s meta-analysis, is **13 or more**. A **4-point** change is Matthey’s reliable-change index ([Cox 2019](https://doi.org/10.1192/bjp.2018.245); [Levis et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7656313/); [Matthey 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15013253/)).

Young Spotlight’s inclusion rule of **55 or higher** on the Public Speaking Anxiety Scale (17 items, total **17–85**, higher worse) falls between the 50th percentile (**52.5**) and the 60th (**57**) of the original US undergraduate sample, whose mean was **51.55** (SD 14.79, median 52.5). The paper does not publish a clinical cutoff. A later Spanish note treats **73** as a suggested cutoff; the 2016 percentile table lists 73 as the **94th** percentile. The norm sample is US undergraduates, so 55 in Norwegian 13–16-year-olds is a study threshold near a US student median, and no adolescent or Norwegian mean was retrieved ([Bartholomay and Houlihan 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.paid.2016.01.026)). Tankevirus’s statement that **58%** of users are high or very high on the SSS-8 (score **≥12**) is several times the German population share of that band, about 5% by the 2014 design and about 10% in the later community sample. The Hamburg outpatient mean of 12.96 sits on the lower edge of “high.” The 58% figure is the write-up’s share; the Tankevirus paper was not re-read for this benchmark. No Norwegian SSS-8 norm was retrieved, and Gierk states that a minimal important difference could not be estimated from the cross-sectional norm study ([Gierk et al. 2014](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/1783305)).

Metric identity changes the label even when the digits look familiar. The self-rated Inventory of Depressive Symptomatology in Oehler et al. 2020 is the **30-item IDS-SR, range 0–84** (28 of 30 items are summed, because appetite and weight increase and decrease are alternatives). Spanish primary-care grades on that metric are none **0–13**, mild **14–25**, moderate **26–38**, severe **39–48**, very severe **above 49**, with **18 or above** described as clinically relevant symptoms. iFightDepression’s baselines of **27.5** (SD 8.9) and **27.9** (SD 8.8) are the low end of moderate. The same integer on the 16-item QIDS-SR (range **0–27**; bands 0–5 none, 6–10 mild, 11–15 moderate, 16–20 severe, 21 or more very severe) would be the top of the very-severe band. The German validation Oehler cites is Drieling, Schärer and Langosch 2007. Grässlin 2004 is not in the extracted reference list. No minimal important difference is specified for the IDS-SR; the trial’s **4.0-point** between-group assumption is a power calculation. Gili’s depressed-patient mean was **34.8** (SD 15) ([Oehler et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7388040/); [Gili et al. 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3188477/); [CADTH QIDS appendix](https://ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK409740/)). DASS-21 severity labels apply after each raw subscale (0–21) is **multiplied by 2**. On that doubled metric, depression is normal at **0–9** and extremely severe at **28 or more**. UK adults averaged a doubled depression score of **5.66** (raw mean 2.83). Outpatients with major depression averaged **29.96** on doubled depression. A raw depression score of 10 is a doubled score of 20, the top of moderate (14–20). Bene in Mente’s figure has to be checked for doubling before it is placed on those bands ([UNSW DASS FAQ](https://dass.psy.unsw.edu.au/DASSFAQ.htm); [Henry and Crawford 2005](https://homepages.abdn.ac.uk/j.crawford/pages/dept/pdfs/BJCP_2005_DASS21.pdf); [Antony et al. 1998](http://imagesrvr.epnet.com/embimages/pdh2/pas/pas102176.pdf)).

Three further student-facing scores are continuous traits with unofficial bands. The PSS-10 runs **0–40**. Cohen published means and no diagnostic cutoff. US adults under 25 in the 2009 probability sample averaged **16.78** (SD 6.86). UK students averaged **19.79** and a 2025 Dutch university survey averaged **20.10** (SD 6.8). The bands **0–13 / 14–26 / 27–40** appear in that Dutch survey (18.5% in the top band) and in other applied papers. They are not in Cohen’s norm paper. GetStarted near 20 matches recent student surveys and sits above the US adult rows ([Cohen and Janicki-Deverts 2012](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/psychology/stress-immunity-disease-lab/publications/scalesmeasurements/pdfs/whos_stressed_jasp_2012.pdf); [Caring Universities 2025](https://moodlift.nl/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Caring-Universities-sixth-survey-report-2025.pdf)). The Irrational Procrastination Scale is nine items from 1 to 5. Published European country means are item means around **2.7–3.2**; a self-selected web sample averaged **3.57**. A total runs from **9 to 45**. The Dutch student survey’s mean of **28.86** is a total (item mean about 3.21). Its split of “problematic” above **31** matches a feedback sheet’s step out of the middle 50% (totals **32–36** are the upper quarter; **37 or more** is the top 10%). A printed 3.2 and a printed 32 are different metrics ([Svartdal et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5005418/); [IPS handout](https://procrastinus.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/irrational-procrastination-scale.pdf); [Caring Universities 2025](https://moodlift.nl/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Caring-Universities-sixth-survey-report-2025.pdf)). The AAQ-II runs **7–49**, higher meaning more psychological inflexibility. Bond’s student means were **17.34** and **21.41**; a substance-misuse treatment sample averaged **28.34**. Bond’s **24–28** band is a preliminary, indirect flag from other instruments’ distress cuts, and Bond says the scale was not built to diagnose. A later student study preferred ROC cuts of **28–32** and called them tentative. Subsequent work finds the items tracking general distress, so a mid-20s score on Bene in Mente reads as “looks distressed” on those study-specific proposals, and there is no consensus clinical cut ([Bond et al. 2011](https://eprints-gro.gold.ac.uk/id/eprint/5531/2/AAQ-II_psychometrics_paper_%28accepted_version%29.pdf); [Ong et al. 2018](https://digitalcommons.usu.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=2764&context=psych_facpub); [Tyndall et al.](https://eprints.chi.ac.uk/id/eprint/3831/1/Accepted%20AAQ-II%20paper%20JCBS.pdf)).

Cool Minds has two anxiety instruments and two competing rule systems. The full Spence Children’s Anxiety Scale scores **38** of 44 items, total **0–114**. Danish school means in the 2024 CEBU guide are girls 7–12 **27.74**, girls 13–17 **26.23**, boys 7–12 **18.44**, boys 13–17 **13.82**. Danish clinical-anxiety means in that guide are **36.49**, **49.58**, **34.42** and **35.68** for the same four cells. Method-C thresholds, where the child looks more like the clinical group than the school group, are **32.64**, **36.19**, **25.48** and **21.61**. The developer’s T-score rule is different: T below **60** is called the normal range (about the bottom 85% of the community tables) and T of 60 or above is “elevated,” not a diagnosis. Australian website means quoted for ages comparable to 7–11 (boys **26.65**, girls **34.02**) are higher than the Danish school means, so the same raw total can be ordinary on the Australian tables and high on the Danish ones ([CEBU scoring guide, November 2024](https://psy.au.dk/fileadmin/PSY/Centre/CEBU/Scoringsredskaber/Opgoerelse_og_scoring_af_SCAS__ny_dansk_version__nov2024.pdf); [SCAS T-score page](https://www.scaswebsite.com/portfolio/scas-child-t-scores/); [Reardon et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6179143/)). The weekly “CAS-8” matches the published description of the **SCAS-C-8** (eight child items, each 0–3, theoretical total **0–24**). In English children aged 7–11 the community mean was **5.97** and the anxiety-clinic mean **9.18**, with a proposed total cutoff of **6.5** (boys 5.5, girls 7.5) and only moderate child-report accuracy. Those norms stop at age 11, Cool Minds extends toward 17, and no Danish short-form mean was found. Item-level identity with Reardon’s eight items was not checked against a Cool Minds item list ([Reardon et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6179143/)).

On a 0–10 numeric rating of symptom intensity or interference, schemes differ by condition. In young people with physical disabilities the average-pain scheme that best tracked interference was **0–3** mild, **4–6** moderate, **7–10** severe. In adults with chronic musculoskeletal pain the best-fitting scheme was **5 or below** mild, **6–7** moderate, **8 or above** severe, and the wider literature places the mild/moderate cut between 3 and 4. eAsy / My Symptoms includes people whose average intensity and average interference are **3 or higher**. On both of those schemes, 3 is still inside mild. It is an entry rule for that intervention ([Miró et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5553452/); [Boonstra et al. 2016](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.01466/full)).

A SUS figure of **34.39** described as **85.98/100** is the 0–40 sum of recoded items. The published conversion multiplies that sum by 2.5. Read on the 0–100 scale, 85.98 sits on the mean of products people called **Excellent** (**85.5**) and above the large-database average of **68** and the “Good” adjective mean of **71.4**. Read as if 34.39 were already out of 100, it would sit next to the “Poor” adjective mean of 35.7. The CSQ-8 development paper was not opened in this search. The assignment’s GetStarted mean of 23.48 was not re-extracted from a paper, so it is not treated here as a verified published score. Satisfaction and usability are not symptom scores ([Bangor, Kortum and Miller 2009](http://uxpajournal.org/wp-content/uploads/pdf/JUS_Bangor_May2009.pdf); [Sauro, MeasuringU](https://measuringu.com/interpret-sus-score/)).

## Cousin instruments supply the wrong normal

Nebolus’s navigation outcome is a **self-developed 15-item** pool on a four-point agreement scale. The trial registry and the 2023 pupil abstract publish no means and no cutoff. The nearest population instrument, HLS19-NAV, is a **different 12-item** scale scored as the percentage of items rated easy or very easy, range **0–100**. Germany’s adult mean on that index is **41.6**, the low end of an eight-country range whose mean of country means was 55.3. That 41.6 describes German adults in 2019–20. It is not a pupil norm, not a student norm, and not a threshold for “adequate” literacy on the Nebolus form. The development paper’s claim that more than 80% of German adults have difficulties navigating health-care information is a person-level statement about an adult survey, and it is not the same statistic as an item-percentage mean of 41.6 ([DRKS00033877](https://drks.de/search/en/trial/DRKS00033877/details); [Dadaczynski et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1055/s-0043-1770574); [HLS19 international report, chapter 10](https://ciencia.ucp.pt/ws/portalfiles/portal/63341554/HLS19_International_Report_242_273.pdf)). Nebolus’s social-distance items are unspecified. The youth adaptation of Link’s five-item scale (1–4, higher meaning greater unwillingness) has no clinical cutoff, and a catalogue score cannot be placed on it until the item set is named ([Yap et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6878256/)).

The same caution covers the other local or unnamed forms. Bene in Mente’s Psychological Well-Being scale is not identified by item count. The 18-item Ryff form (three items on each of six dimensions) is the usual brief survey; subscales on a 7-point scale run **3–21**, and a 6-point Swedish version runs **18–108** as a total. No Italian student mean, no clinical cut, and no confirmation of the 84-, 42-, 18- or 9-item form were retrieved, so no numeric normal is stated here ([SPARQ 18-item sheet](https://datashare.ed.ac.uk/bitstream/handle/10283/8553/Ryff-18.pdf); [Garcia et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10580072/)). The SInAPSi Academic Engagement Scale is a published 29-item, six-factor instrument. Naples science-student factor means sit mostly around **3 to 4** (belonging about 4.08, persistence about 4.20, professors about 3.22 in first years). The paper reports no total-score benchmark and no clinical cut, and the response anchors were not in the retrieved text ([Freda et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12144-021-02189-2)). Deep Breath’s Mental Health Literacy Scale, where the full 35-item original is used, runs **35–160**. The 2015 mean of **127.38** (SD 12.63) is first-year Australian psychology students, whom the authors say may have inflated literacy relative to a broader community. Mental-health professionals averaged **145.49**. There is no clinical cutoff. A partial adaptation cannot be placed on 35–160 unless all 35 items and the original scoring were kept ([O’Connor and Casey 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.psychres.2015.05.064)).

Mindhelper’s vignette General Help-Seeking Questionnaire is a set of **1–7** intentions, one per help source, with no total and no clinical cut. In the original Australian high-school sample, personal-emotional problems were directed to a friend (**5.13**), a parent (**4.84**) and a partner (**4.77**); a GP was **2.73** and a mental-health professional **2.68**. “Would not seek help” averaged **2.86** and is scored in the same direction as the other rows. An English-language Chilean adaptation prints the anchors reversed relative to Wilson’s form, so Chilean and Australian numbers should not be averaged until the Spanish wording is checked. No Danish youth means were retrieved ([Wilson et al.](https://josephciarrochi.com/downloads/2005%20-%20Measuring%20help-seeking%20intentions%20Properties%20of%20the%20general%20help-seeking%20questionnaire.pdf)).

Mindliven’s occupational and process measures are benchmarks of a different kind. The HSE Management Standards Indicator Tool scores seven areas from **1 to 5**, and a **lower** score means worse working conditions. Colour comes from percentiles that differ by standard and by which benchmark set is used. On the older 136-organisation cuts, Demands is urgent below **2.9387**, needs improvement from there up to **3.1024**, and is in the top quintile from **3.2937**. The 2026 all-industry report (83,452 responses) puts the Demands 20th percentile at **3.06**, next to that older median, with an all-industry Demands mean of **3.25**. Job Demands and Job Resources, if collapsed from the seven HSE scores, have no official percentile. Role and peer support sit higher in raw numbers than Demands and Change, and the manual says a lower average is not automatically a worse percentile ([HSE Analysis Tool user manual](https://www.alanbradshaw.uk/uploads/2/7/0/6/2706840/analysis_tool_manual.pdf); [HSE Books 2026 benchmarking report](https://books.hse.tso.co.uk/sites/default/files/2026-09/The%202026%20HSE%20Benchmarking%20Report.pdf)). “Reappraisal and suppression” matches the Emotion Regulation Questionnaire if that is the scale Mindliven used. The ERQ is ten items from 1 to 7. US student item-means were about **4.60** for reappraisal in both sexes, and **3.64** for men and **3.14** for women on suppression. German community suppression figures of about **18** are sums (suppression sums run 4–28), not 1–7 means. There is no clinical cutoff, and the intervention text does not name the ERQ ([Gross and John 2003](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.85.2.348); [Wiltink et al. 2011](https://www.egms.de/static/de/journals/psm/2011-8/psm000078.shtml)). No scored instrument named REST@WORK was found with sample means. The 2026 Restorativeness at Work Scale (R@WS) is a different name, and its abstract reports no means. The Nature Contact Questionnaire’s development table gives a **10-item** total mean of **22.5** (SD 8.3) in office staff. A companion paper describes a **16-item** total from **16 to 96**. Those two totals are not the same score, and neither is a symptom norm ([Largo-Wight et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.3233/WOR-2011-1253); [Largo-Wight et al., Public Health Reports](https://ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3072911/)).

The UEQ-S, used by Nebolus, is a usability measure on a **−3 to +3** metric. A benchmark transformed from the longer UEQ labels an overall mean above **1.58** as excellent (best 10% of products), **1.40 to 1.58** as good, and below **0.68** as bad (worst 25%). A positive user-experience mean says nothing about navigation literacy or stigma ([Hinderks, Schrepp and Thomaschewski 2018](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2018/71883/71883.pdf)).

Individual change, where a sourced figure exists, is a third number and should stay labelled as change. Five points is the PHQ-9 figure most often treated as meaningful for an individual, from measurement error in older US primary-care patients, while English therapy services have used six on the PHQ-9 and four on the GAD-7 ([Löwe et al. 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15550799/); [Journal of Clinical Epidemiology 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8485844/)). Four points is also the EPDS reliable-change index ([Matthey 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15013253/)). None of those deltas transfer to the PHQ-4, the SMFQ, the SSS-8, the DASS-21, the IDS-SR, or the PSS-10. No minimal important change was retrieved for those scales.

## Conclusion

The editorial unit is the scored metric, not the name printed in a submission. Once that metric is fixed, the intervention samples that published their own baselines already sit where the instrument literature says a selected group should sit: mild PHQ-9 and low-moderate IDS-SR in iFightDepression, a CES-D in the low 30s and a definite-case HADS anxiety score in Master Your Mood, an EQ-VAS next to the Norwegian depression-and-anxiety mean in Tankevirus, and a PHQ-4 mix far heavier in the severe band than US primary care. Setting those baselines beside the general-population mean shows the gap the intervention is for. Setting them beside the screening cut, or beside a cousin instrument, makes a clinical group look only mildly off and makes a local questionnaire look normed.

Where this search found no external normal — Nebolus’s 15 navigation items, the unidentified Ryff form, REST@WORK, adolescent or Norwegian PSAS, Danish CAS-8, and Italian students on several Bene in Mente scales — the catalogue line that matches the evidence is that no published normal was retrieved. Borrowing HLS19-NAV’s 41.6, a US undergraduate median, or an undoubled DASS band fills the cell with a number from a different instrument. Country and age still move the anchors that do exist: Danish SCAS school means run lower than the Australian website means, German WHO-5 and EQ-VAS norms are the right neighbourhood for Nebolus and Tankevirus and are not youth norms, and the HSE percentile set from 136 organisations will paint a different colour from the 2026 all-industry set on the same workplace mean.
